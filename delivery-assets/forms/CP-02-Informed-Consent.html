Superficial · Medium · Deep Peels
Procedure Description

A chemical peel is a controlled application of a chemical exfoliating solution to the skin, which causes the outermost layers to separate and eventually peel off. This process stimulates new cell growth, collagen production, and results in smoother, more evenly toned skin. The depth of the peel depends on the type and concentration of the solution used, the number of layers applied, and the duration of contact with the skin. Your provider will select the appropriate peel based on your skin type, concerns, and treatment goals.

Peel Type Selection
Provider will select the peel category and active ingredient(s) to be used during treatment.
Superficial — AHA, BHA, lactic, glycolic, mandelic
Medium — TCA 15–35%, Jessner's solution
Deep — TCA 50%+, phenol-based
Benefits & Risks
Expected Benefits Improved skin tone and texture · Reduction of fine lines and wrinkles · Improvement in acne and blemish appearance · Reduction of hyperpigmentation, sun spots, and melasma · Stimulation of cellular turnover and collagen synthesis · Enhanced product penetration and skincare efficacy
Common Risks Redness and erythema · Stinging or burning sensation during application · Peeling, flaking, and dryness · Temporary skin sensitivity · Tightness of treated skin
Less Common Risks Prolonged erythema · Blistering or crusting · Temporary post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation · Skin breakout or purging reaction
Rare Risks Scarring (hypertrophic or atrophic) · Permanent hypopigmentation · Bacterial, viral, or fungal infection · Reactivation of herpes simplex (cold sores) · Allergic or hypersensitivity reaction

Alternatives & No-Guarantee Statement

Alternatives to chemical peels include microdermabrasion, laser skin resurfacing, microneedling, topical retinoid therapy, and no treatment. Your provider has discussed these options with you.

There is no guarantee of specific results from chemical peel treatment. Individual outcomes vary based on skin type, condition severity, adherence to pre- and post-treatment instructions, and biological response. Additional treatments may be necessary to achieve desired results.
