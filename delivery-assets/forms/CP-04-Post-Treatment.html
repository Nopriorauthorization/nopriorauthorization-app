Post-Treatment Aftercare
Chemical Peel
Proper aftercare is critical to your healing and results. Please follow these instructions carefully. Contact the clinic immediately if you experience any signs of infection or unusual symptoms.
Immediately After Treatment
  • Redness, tightness, and a mild stinging sensation are normal and may last several hours.
  • Your skin may feel warm, similar to a sunburn — this is expected.
  • Treated skin may appear darker or develop a brownish tint before peeling begins; this is a normal part of the exfoliation process.
  • Do not apply any products to your skin unless specifically instructed by your provider.
Days 1–3: Gentle Care Phase
  • Cleanse gently — use only a mild, non-foaming cleanser (no scrubbing, no washcloths, no cleansing brushes). Pat dry with a clean towel.
  • Moisturize liberally — apply the recommended post-peel moisturizer or healing balm generously and frequently to prevent excessive dryness and cracking.
  • SPF 30+ before any sun exposure — apply a broad-spectrum mineral sunscreen every morning, even if staying indoors near windows. Reapply every 2 hours if outdoors.
  • Do not touch or rub your face — avoid unnecessary contact with the treated area. Hands carry bacteria that can cause infection.
  • No active ingredients — do not use retinoids, AHAs, BHAs, vitamin C, benzoyl peroxide, or any exfoliating products.
  • Sleep elevated — if swelling is present, sleep with your head elevated on an extra pillow for the first 1–2 nights.
Days 3–7: Peeling Phase
  • DO NOT PICK, PEEL, or SCRATCH — peeling and flaking will begin naturally. Pulling or picking at peeling skin can cause scarring, infection, or post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.
  • Continue generous moisturizing — keeping skin hydrated helps manage peeling and reduces tightness and discomfort.
  • Avoid makeup on actively peeling areas — if possible, allow skin to breathe. If makeup is necessary, use only mineral-based, non-comedogenic products.
  • No sweating or vigorous exercise — avoid strenuous activity, hot yoga, and heavy workouts for the first 3 days to prevent irritation and excess perspiration on healing skin.
  • No swimming, hot tubs, or saunas — avoid chlorinated water, steam rooms, and hot tubs for a minimum of 7 days. These environments increase infection risk and irritation.
Week 2+: Recovery & Reintroduction
  • Peeling should be complete by the end of week 2 for most superficial and medium-depth peels. Deep peels may take longer.
  • Gradually reintroduce active products — add back one product at a time, starting with the lowest concentration. Wait 2–3 days between each reintroduction to monitor for sensitivity.
  • Continue daily SPF for a minimum of 6 weeks — your new skin is significantly more vulnerable to UV damage and hyperpigmentation during this period.
  • Your skin will appear brighter, smoother, and more evenly toned as healing completes.
What's Normal Redness lasting 3–7 days (medium-depth peels may persist longer) · Skin tightness and mild discomfort · Mild swelling, especially around eyes and jawline · Sheet-like or flaky peeling · Temporary darkening of treated skin before peeling · Itching as skin heals
When to Call the Clinic Blistering or open, weeping wounds · Oozing, yellow crusting, or pus (signs of infection) · Increasing pain rather than improvement · Fever above 100.4°F (38°C) · Severe or prolonged swelling beyond 72 hours · Signs of allergic reaction (hives, difficulty breathing, swelling of lips/throat) · Cold sore outbreak on treated area
