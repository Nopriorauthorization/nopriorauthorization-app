Part 1 — Breach Notification Policy
1. Purpose & Scope

This policy establishes the procedures Hello Gorgeous Med Spa ("the Practice") will follow to identify, investigate, and report breaches of unsecured protected health information (PHI) in compliance with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), the Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health (HITECH) Act, and the Omnibus Rule (45 CFR §§ 164.400–414). This policy applies to all workforce members, including employees, volunteers, trainees, contractors, and any other persons whose conduct is under the direct control of the Practice.

2. Definition of a Breach

A breach is defined as the acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of PHI in a manner not permitted under the HIPAA Privacy Rule that compromises the security or privacy of the PHI. A breach is presumed to have occurred unless the Practice demonstrates that there is a low probability that the PHI has been compromised, based on a documented risk assessment.

Exceptions to the definition of breach:

• Unintentional acquisition, access, or use of PHI by a workforce member acting in good faith and within the scope of authority, provided the information is not further used or disclosed in an impermissible manner.
• Inadvertent disclosure by an authorized person to another authorized person at the same covered entity or business associate, provided the information is not further used or disclosed in an impermissible manner.
• A disclosure where the Practice has a good faith belief that the unauthorized person to whom the disclosure was made would not reasonably have been able to retain the information.
3. Risk Assessment Factors

When a potential breach is identified, the Practice shall conduct a risk assessment evaluating at minimum the following four factors:

FactorAssessment Criteria
Nature and extent of PHITypes of identifiers involved, likelihood of re-identification, and sensitivity of the information (e.g., SSN, diagnosis, financial data)
Unauthorized personIdentity of the unauthorized person who used the PHI or to whom the disclosure was made, and whether that person has obligations to protect PHI
Acquisition or viewingWhether the PHI was actually acquired or viewed, as opposed to the opportunity to do so (e.g., returned unopened mail vs. confirmed access)
Extent of risk mitigationSteps taken to mitigate the risk, including obtaining assurances from the recipient that the information will not be further used or disclosed, or that it has been destroyed
4. Notification Requirements

Timeline: Notification must be made without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 calendar days from the date the breach is discovered. A breach is considered "discovered" on the first day it is known or should reasonably have been known by any workforce member.

Individual Notification: Written notice must be sent by first-class mail to the last known address of each affected individual (or by email if the individual has agreed to electronic notice). The notification must include: a description of the breach, the types of PHI involved, steps individuals should take to protect themselves, a description of the Practice's mitigation efforts, and contact information for further questions.

Media Notification: If a breach affects 500 or more individuals in a single state or jurisdiction, the Practice must notify prominent media outlets serving that area within 60 days of discovery.

HHS Notification: All breaches must be reported to the Secretary of HHS. Breaches affecting 500 or more individuals must be reported within 60 days. Breaches affecting fewer than 500 individuals may be reported annually within 60 days of the end of the calendar year in which they were discovered.

5. Staff Reporting Procedure
Immediate action required: Any workforce member who suspects or discovers a potential breach of PHI must report it to the Privacy Officer within 24 hours of discovery. Failure to report a suspected breach may result in disciplinary action.

Upon receiving a report, the Privacy Officer shall: (1) document the initial report; (2) initiate an investigation within 48 hours; (3) conduct the four-factor risk assessment; (4) determine whether breach notification is required; (5) issue required notifications within the mandated timeline; and (6) document all actions taken and maintain records for a minimum of six years.
