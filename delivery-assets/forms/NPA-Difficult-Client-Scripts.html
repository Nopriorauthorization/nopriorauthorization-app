From Danielle
"Nobody prepares you for the moment a client looks you in the eye and says they hate their results. Or the first time someone demands a refund after you did everything right. I've had every one of these conversations. Some I handled well. Some I didn't. These scripts are what I wish I'd had — the words that de-escalate, protect your business, and let you keep your integrity at the same time."

The 3 rules behind every script

1. Acknowledge before you defend. The client needs to feel heard before they can hear anything you say. 2. Never apologize for the outcome — acknowledge the feeling. "I'm sorry you're unhappy" is not an admission of wrongdoing. 3. Move the conversation offline as fast as possible. Public arguments with clients never end well for the provider.

Scenarios 01–05
Unhappy Clients & Complaints
The conversations that feel the most personal — and require the most care

01 — "I hate my results" — in person

In-Clinic
Client sits up from the treatment chair, looks in the mirror, and immediately expresses disappointment or distress about what they see.
What to say
I hear you, and I appreciate you telling me right now instead of leaving without saying anything. Your feelings matter to me. Before we talk through it, can I ask — what specifically isn't feeling right? I want to make sure I understand exactly what you're seeing. [Listen fully.] Here's what I want you to know: your results are still developing and will continue to change over the next 14 days. What you're seeing right now isn't your final result. Can we schedule your follow-up appointment for two weeks from today so I can assess everything properly — and so I can take care of you the right way?
Why this works: You acknowledged the feeling immediately, asked clarifying questions before defending, reminded them of the timeline without dismissing their concern, and moved toward a solution. You did not apologize for the treatment outcome.

02 — "I hate my results" — via text

Text / DM
Client sends an upset text or DM about their results — usually in the first few days when swelling is still present.
What to say
Hi [Name] — I'm so glad you reached out and I'm sorry to hear you're feeling this way. I take every client's experience seriously and I want to make this right. Can we connect by phone today so I can hear exactly what you're experiencing? Results do continue to develop over the next 14 days and I want to make sure we assess everything properly. Text me back a good time to call — I want to take care of you.
Never try to resolve a serious complaint over text. Move it to a phone call immediately. Texts get screenshotted. Your words on the phone are much harder to take out of context.

03 — The refund demand

High Stakes
Client demands a full or partial refund, either in person or in writing.
What to say — phone or in person
I hear that you're unhappy and I genuinely want to address your concerns. I'm not in a position to offer a refund for services that were rendered — that's our policy, which you'll also find in the consent form you signed. What I can do is make sure we take proper care of you going forward. I'd like to schedule a complimentary follow-up so I can see exactly what's happening and discuss what options we have from here. My goal is for you to feel taken care of — not to end this experience with you frustrated. Can we do that?
Your policy on refunds should be in writing — on your consent form and your website. Never offer a refund in the heat of the moment. Offer a follow-up first. Many refund demands resolve completely once the client feels heard and cared for.

04 — The bad Google review response

Public Response
A negative or inaccurate Google review appears. You need to respond professionally — publicly, in writing, forever.
Public response script
Thank you for sharing your experience. We take every piece of feedback seriously and we're genuinely sorry to hear your visit didn't meet your expectations. We'd love the opportunity to connect with you directly so we can better understand your experience and see how we can make it right. Please reach out to us at [phone/email] — we'd appreciate the chance to speak with you personally. Your satisfaction truly matters to us.
Never argue publicly. Never explain what happened publicly. Never mention HIPAA or patient privacy in your response — it signals to readers that you can't defend the situation. Invite them offline. Every potential patient reading your reviews is watching how you handle this more than they're reading the complaint itself.

05 — The client who blames you for a complication

Liability Risk
Client contacts you saying they had a reaction, bruising, swelling, or other complication they believe was caused by your treatment.
What to say
I'm really glad you contacted me and I want to hear everything you're experiencing. Can you describe exactly what you're seeing or feeling? [Listen.] I want to see you in person as soon as possible — today if you can. Don't apply anything new to the area. I'll assess what's happening and make sure you're taken care of. Can you come in this afternoon? If you're experiencing severe pain, vision changes, or difficulty breathing — please go to an emergency room immediately and call me on the way.
Do not admit liability. Do not speculate about cause over text or phone. Do get them in front of you as fast as possible. Document everything. Contact your malpractice carrier if you believe this may escalate to a formal complaint.
Scenarios 06–10
Boundaries, Policies & Expectations
The conversations where you have to hold the line — professionally

06 — The repeat no-show

Policy Enforcement
A client has no-showed or cancelled last-minute for the second or third time.
What to say
Hi [Name] — I wanted to reach out personally about your missed appointment. I value you as a client and I want to continue taking care of you. At the same time, I have to be transparent — when an appointment isn't cancelled with adequate notice, that time can't be filled by another patient who needs it, and it does affect our small practice. Going forward, I'll need a card on file to hold your appointments and our cancellation policy will apply. I wanted to tell you directly rather than just send a policy email. Can we get you rescheduled?
Personal, direct, not punitive — but clear. Framing it as protecting other patients (true) is more effective than "it costs us money." Have your cancellation policy in writing before this conversation happens.

07 — The price negotiator

Boundaries
Client asks for a discount, offers to pay less, or says they can get it cheaper somewhere else.
What to say
I completely understand that price matters — it does for everyone. What I can tell you is that our pricing reflects the quality of products we use, the training and credentials behind every treatment, and the level of care you receive at every visit. We don't discount our services because we don't cut corners on any of those things. What I can do is tell you about our membership program — which is the best way to get consistent care at the most value. Would that be helpful?
Never apologize for your prices. Never negotiate. Pivot to the membership as your value alternative. A client who starts negotiating before they've even had a treatment will negotiate at every visit.

08 — The client who wants free touch-ups

Boundaries
Client returns claiming they need a free touch-up — either outside the complimentary 2-week window or for something that isn't a clinical indication for a touch-up.
What to say
I really appreciate you coming back and I want to take a look at everything. [Assess.] Here's what I'm seeing — [clinical finding]. Our complimentary touch-up window is within 14 days of your original treatment and is for clinical asymmetry or placement correction. What you're describing is [explanation]. I want to take care of you — here's what I recommend and what that looks like in terms of your next appointment.
Assess first, then have the conversation. If it's within your complimentary window and is a legitimate clinical finding — take care of it graciously, no charge. If it's outside the window or is a preference issue — explain clearly and move toward a paid appointment.

09 — The angry Instagram DM

Social Media
Client sends an upset, accusatory, or threatening direct message on Instagram or another social platform.
What to say
Hi [Name] — I received your message and I want to make sure I give your concerns the attention they deserve. Can I give you a call? This is a conversation I want to have directly — not over DM. My number is [phone]. I'll be available [time]. I genuinely want to understand what happened and talk through it with you.
Never respond with anything substantive over social media DMs. Move to phone immediately. Screenshot every DM you receive from a dissatisfied client before responding. If a client threatens to "expose" you or post publicly — do not engage with that threat. Contact your attorney.

10 — The client who didn't follow aftercare

Documentation
Client is unhappy with their results and upon questioning you discover they exercised immediately after Botox, got a spray tan after filler, picked at their microneedling, etc.
What to say
Thank you for being honest with me — I know it's not easy to share that. Here's the thing: the aftercare instructions I sent aren't arbitrary — they directly impact your results. What you're experiencing is likely connected to [specific aftercare issue]. I want to help you get the best possible outcome from here — so let's talk about what we can do going forward and how we prevent this next time. Going forward I'll also be checking in at [timeframe] specifically to make sure you're on track.
Do not blame or shame. Explain the connection clinically. This is also why documenting that you provided aftercare instructions is so important — if a client claims you didn't tell them, your documentation protects you.
Scenarios 11–15
The Hard Conversations
Firing clients, family pressure, false information, and more

11 — Firing a client professionally

Client Dismissal
A client has been consistently disrespectful, has crossed a boundary repeatedly, or the relationship is no longer serving either party.
Written communication — letter or email
Dear [Name] — I'm reaching out to let you know that after reflection, I feel that our practice may not be the best fit for your needs going forward. This is never an easy decision, and it's not one I make lightly. Effective [date 30 days out], we will no longer be able to schedule future appointments for you. I want to make sure this transition is smooth — if you are in the middle of a treatment series, I will [complete X / refer you to Y]. I wish you all the best and hope you find the right provider for your goals.
Always give adequate notice (30 days minimum for medical patients). Do not cite specific incidents in the dismissal letter — keep it professional and brief. Send via certified mail if the situation has been contentious. Document everything. For medical patients, check your state's patient abandonment rules before dismissing.

12 — The friend/family discount pressure

Personal Boundaries
A friend, family member, or their referral expects significant discounts or free services because of the relationship.
What to say
I love that you trust me with this — truly. Here's the thing I've learned the hard way: when I mix personal relationships with discounted services, it gets complicated for both of us. I value our relationship too much to let business complicate it. What I can do is make sure you always feel taken care of — but I keep my pricing consistent because it's the only way I can run this practice fairly and keep the lights on. I'd love to see you — at my regular rates — any time.
Said warmly and with love — not defensively. The framing of protecting the relationship is both true and more effective than talking about policy. Have this conversation once, clearly, and most people will respect it.

13 — "I can get it cheaper at [other place]"

Value Defense
Client uses a cheaper competitor to pressure you to match a lower price.
What to say
I think that's worth exploring — and I want you to make the right decision for yourself. What I'd encourage you to ask is: what's included in that price? What credentials does the injector hold? What product are they using and where do they source it? At Hello Gorgeous, [your practice name], you know exactly what you're getting — the product, the credentials, the follow-up care, and me. If the other option checks all those boxes for you too, I completely understand. I just want you to be making a fully informed comparison.
Never trash a competitor by name. Ask the questions that highlight your value without saying "they're worse than us." Let the client draw their own conclusions. Clients who leave for price almost always come back — and they come back loyal.

14 — The cancellation fee conversation

Policy Enforcement
Client is upset about being charged a cancellation or no-show fee.
What to say
I completely understand that things come up — and I genuinely appreciate that you've been a client with us. The cancellation policy exists because when an appointment isn't cancelled with adequate notice, that time cannot be filled by another patient who needs it — and as a small practice, that time is how we keep our doors open. That fee was communicated at booking and is something all our clients agree to. I value your business and I don't want this to affect our relationship — can we get you rescheduled?
Hold the fee. Waiving it once teaches clients that the policy is negotiable. The key is empathy + firmness + pivot to the future relationship. If you waive it, do so as a one-time professional courtesy — not as a capitulation.

15 — The client posting false information publicly

Legal Risk
A client is publicly posting inaccurate, misleading, or defamatory statements about your practice on social media or review platforms.
What to say — direct outreach
Hi [Name] — I've seen your posts and I want to reach out directly before this goes any further. I understand you're frustrated and I genuinely want to resolve this — but some of what's been shared publicly isn't accurate, and I'd really like the opportunity to talk through what actually happened. Can we connect by phone today? I want to make this right if there's a way to do that. My number is [phone].
Do NOT respond publicly to false claims with specific details — you risk a HIPAA violation if you reference their treatment even to correct false information. Document everything with screenshots and dates. If the posts are genuinely defamatory (false statements of fact, not just negative opinions), consult an attorney about your options. Do not threaten legal action in your first outreach.
