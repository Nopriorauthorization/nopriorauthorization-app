KEY
Darker skin tones (Fitzpatrick V–VI) require longer wavelengths (Nd:YAG 1064nm preferred). Shorter wavelengths absorbed by epidermal melanin = burn and PIH risk. Always assess skin type before treating.

PATCH
Test patch mandatory for every new client — especially for IPL. Test area: inconspicuous location matching treatment area. Assess at 24–48 hours before treating larger area. Document settings used.
🦵 Permanent Hair Removal — Laser Reference
HOW
Laser targets melanin in the hair follicle — selectively heats and destroys follicle while sparing surrounding tissue (selective photothermolysis). Only effective on hair in anagen (active growth) phase.

NO TREAT
Blonde, red, gray, or white hair cannot be treated with laser. No melanin = no target = no result. Electrolysis is the only option for light/gray hair. Set expectations clearly at consultation.
INTERVALS
Face: 4 weeks. Body: 6–8 weeks. Must align with anagen cycle. Shave 24–48hrs before treatment — do not wax or pluck (removes follicle target). No tan 4 weeks before.

🚫 Contraindications — All Laser/IPL
ABS
Absolute: Active tan or sunburn (4 weeks no sun before treatment). Active skin infection in treatment area. Photosensitizing medications (see below). Pregnancy. History of keloid scarring (ablative treatments). Active HSV in treatment area.
ABS
Absolute — pigment treatments: Active melasma without physician oversight. Vitiligo (laser can expand lesions). Active autoimmune conditions affecting skin.
MEDS
Photosensitizing medications — hold or caution: Doxycycline, tetracycline, minocycline. Accutane (6 months minimum post-course before any laser). St. John's Wort. Certain antifungals. Amiodarone. Screen all medications at intake.
REL
Relative: Fitzpatrick V–VI (use appropriate wavelength). History of PIH. Darker skin phototype in summer. PCOS (facial laser hair — hormones regrow hair — manage expectations). Immunosuppression.
ACCUTANE
Accutane: minimum 6 months after last dose before ANY ablative or aggressive laser treatment. 12 months preferred. Impairs wound healing — risk of scarring and poor outcomes.
⚠️ Complication Management
PIH
Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation: Most common complication. Risk: darker skin tones, overtreated area, sun exposure post-treatment. Management: strict SPF 50+, hydroquinone 4%, azelaic acid. May take 3–6 months to resolve.
BURN
Laser burn/blistering: Cool compress immediately. Silvadene cream or non-adherent dressing. Keep moist, clean. Do not pop blisters. Refer to MD if severe or large area. Document settings used.
PURPURA
Purpura (bruising) — vascular treatments: Expected with some vascular lasers. Resolves in 7–14 days. Arnica gel. Avoid blood thinners. Warn patient before treatment — consent.
PARADOX
Paradoxical hypertrichosis: Hair stimulation instead of removal — more common with IPL on darker skin or low fluence. Switch to true laser (diode/Nd:YAG). Increase fluence. Can occur with hormonal conditions (PCOS).
FLARE
HSV reactivation: Laser triggers cold sore outbreak. Prescribe valacyclovir prophylaxis before any facial laser on clients with HSV history. 500mg BID starting day before, 5 days total.
☀️ Sun Safety Protocol — Non-Negotiable
PRE
No sun/tanning 4 weeks before treatment. No self-tanner 2 weeks before. No spray tan. Tan skin = increased melanin = burn risk + PIH risk. Reschedule if client is tanned — no exceptions.
POST
SPF 50+ broad-spectrum daily for minimum 4 weeks post-treatment. No direct sun exposure for 2 weeks. Hat if outdoors. Sun exposure post-laser = PIH and undoes results. This is non-negotiable.
YEAR
Clients receiving laser treatments should be on year-round daily SPF 50+. This is your most important patient education point. Recommend a medical-grade zinc-based SPF at every appointment.
DOCUMENT
Document sun avoidance counseling in every chart note. If client is tanned — document refusal to treat and reason. Protects you legally if complications occur from non-compliant sun exposure.
📋 Pre & Post Treatment Protocols
PRE
All laser/IPL: No tan 4 weeks. No photosensitizing meds. No Accutane 6–12 months. Shave area (hair removal). Cleanse skin — no makeup, SPF, or products. Remove jewelry/metal near area. Eye protection mandatory.
DAY OF
Apply topical numbing 30–60 min before if needed (LMX 4% or EMLA). Cleanse. Test patch if new settings. Photograph treatment area before service. Use cooling device during treatment if available.
POST
Cool compress or cooling gel immediately post-treatment. Aloe vera. Gentle cleanser only for 24–48 hours. No heat (exercise, sauna, hot shower) for 24–48 hours. SPF 50+ immediately and daily. No exfoliants for 1 week.
HAIR
Post laser hair removal: Hair will appear to grow for 1–3 weeks then shed. This is normal — it's the treated follicle pushing the dead hair out. Do not wax or pluck — shave only between sessions.
🧠 High-Yield Laser/IPL Pearls
★
Fitzpatrick assessment before every treatment. Skin type can change seasonally. A client who was Fitz III in winter may be Fitz IV in summer after vacation.
★
Start conservative — you can always go higher. You cannot un-burn a client. Low fluence + multiple sessions = better outcomes than aggressive single treatment.
★
Test patch for every new client. 24–48 hour assessment before treating full area. This is your most important risk reduction step.
★
Melasma + laser = extreme caution. Laser can trigger and worsen melasma. Low fluence, longer intervals, and topical prep (HQ + retinol) essential. Many melasma cases are better served by topicals alone.
★
Laser hair removal is a series — not a one-time treatment. Set expectations at consultation: 6–8 sessions minimum, maintenance may be needed annually. PCOS patients need ongoing treatment.
★
Eye protection mandatory for everyone in the room — client, technician, and anyone else present. Wavelength-appropriate safety eyewear. Non-negotiable for every laser and IPL treatment.
★
SPF 50+ is the most important product you sell. Every laser client should be on daily SPF 50+. No exceptions. It protects results and prevents complications. Recommend and retail it every single visit.
