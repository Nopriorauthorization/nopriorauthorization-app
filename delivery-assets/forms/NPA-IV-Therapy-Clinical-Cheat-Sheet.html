🧬 NAD+ Protocol — Special Considerations
WHAT
Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide. Coenzyme involved in cellular energy, DNA repair, sirtuin activation. Declines with age. IV delivery bypasses absorption limitations of oral NMN/NR.
DoseVolumeRun TimeIndication
250mg250mL NS2 hrs minimumIntro / sensitive patients
500mg250mL NS2.5–3 hrsStandard dose
750mg500mL NS3–4 hrsAnti-aging / cognitive
1000mg500mL NS4+ hrsAddiction support, advanced
SIDE FX
Rate-dependent side effects: Chest tightness, nausea, flushing, cramping — all from infusing too fast. Slow the rate, do not stop. These resolve when rate decreases. Always run slow.
SERIES
Optimal protocol: 4 infusions over 2 weeks for loading, then monthly maintenance. Anti-addiction protocols: daily x 10 days under medical supervision.
STORAGE
Refrigerate unopened. Light-sensitive — use amber bag or wrap in foil. Once mixed, infuse within 6 hours. Do not freeze.
🚫 Contraindications
ABS
Absolute: Active infection/fever. Congestive heart failure (fluid overload risk). Severe renal failure (fluid + electrolyte management). Known allergy to any component.
ABS
High-dose Vitamin C: G6PD deficiency (hemolytic anemia risk). Oxalate kidney stones history. Renal impairment. Test G6PD before any dose >10g.
REL
Relative: Uncontrolled HTN — monitor BP before and during. Pregnancy — consult OB first. Diabetes — monitor glucose with dextrose-containing solutions.
REL
Cardiac history: Magnesium and calcium IV require slower rates. QT prolongation risk with Zofran — screen EKG history. Obtain cardiac clearance for high-dose infusions.
SCREEN
Always screen: CHF, renal disease, current medications, allergies, recent labs if available. Vital signs before every infusion.
✅ Pre-Treatment Protocol
VITALS
BP, HR, temp, SpO2 before every infusion. Document in chart. If BP >160/100 — consult before proceeding with high-volume infusions.
HYDRATE
Instruct patient: drink 16–32oz water before arrival. Well-hydrated veins = easier access. Fasting increases vasovagal risk.
EAT
Eat before IV — especially NAD+. Empty stomach worsens nausea side effects from NAD+, high-dose Vit C, and magnesium. Light meal 1–2hrs before.
CONSENT
Signed consent for each infusion or standing consent for series. Document indication, components, patient education provided.
LABS
High-dose Vit C: G6PD required. Consider CMP for patients with unknown renal status. Not required for standard drips in healthy patients.
⚠️ IV Complication Management
INFILTRA
Infiltration (fluid in tissue): Stop infusion immediately. Remove catheter. Elevate extremity. Warm compress for most solutions. Cold compress for vasoconstrictors. Document site, estimate volume extravasated.
PHLEBITIS
Phlebitis: Redness, warmth, pain along vein. Remove catheter. Warm moist compress. Elevate. Document. Monitor for infection. Do not reuse same vein for 48hrs.
VASOVAGAL
Vasovagal syncope: Pale, diaphoretic, bradycardic, presyncopal. Trendelenburg position. Cool cloth. Ammonia capsule. Monitor vitals. Do not leave patient alone. 95% resolve quickly.
ALLERGIC
Allergic reaction: Urticaria, pruritis, bronchospasm. STOP infusion. Benadryl 25–50mg IV. Epinephrine 0.3mg IM for anaphylaxis. Call 911. Have epi on-site always.
AIR EMBOL
Air embolism prevention: Prime tubing completely. Use Luer-lock connections. Monitor bag — do not run dry. Clamp line immediately when bag empty. Position patient if suspected: left lateral decubitus, Trendelenburg.
💉 IV Insertion — Technique Guide
SITES
Preferred sites: Median cubital (AC), cephalic, basilic veins. Forearm preferred over hand for comfort and duration. Avoid lower extremities, areas of infection, AV fistulas, mastectomy side.
GAUGE
22g — standard wellness infusions, most patients. 20g — faster flow needed, larger volumes. 24g — fragile/small veins, elderly, pediatric.
TECHNIQUE
Apply tourniquet 3–4" above site. Palpate and visualize. Clean with alcohol — dry 30 sec. Bevel up at 15–30°. Advance until flash. Lower angle, advance catheter. Release tourniquet. Remove needle. Connect tubing. Secure.
DIFFICULT
Difficult access: Warm compress/warm pack 5–10 min first. Hydrate patient. Gravity assist — arm below heart. Gentle fist pumping. Vein finder device. Max 2 attempts per provider.
DWELL TIME
72–96hr max dwell per CDC guidelines for peripheral IV. For wellness/spa IV — remove after each infusion. New site each visit. Document site used.

🧠 High-Yield Clinical Pearls
★
Glutathione always last. Push after the bag completes — never add to IV bag. Oxidizes immediately in solution.
★
NAD+ side effects are rate-dependent. Slow the drip — don't stop it. All symptoms resolve with reduced rate.
★
G6PD test before high-dose Vit C. Hemolytic anemia in G6PD-deficient patients is life-threatening. Non-negotiable.
★
Eat before IV. Especially NAD+. Empty stomach significantly worsens side effects. Make it a standing pre-treatment instruction.
★
Epinephrine on-site always. Anaphylaxis is rare but fast. 0.3mg EpiPen in every treatment room.
★
Magnesium too fast = hypotension. Always run magnesium additives over the full bag duration — never bolus.
★
Document every infusion: Site, gauge, components, volume, run time, patient tolerance, vitals pre/post. Protects you legally.
★
Biotin interferes with thyroid labs. Hold biotin-containing IVs for 48hrs before thyroid blood draw. Educate patients.

📋 Documentation Checklist
BEFORE
Patient name + DOB verified. Consent signed. Allergy review completed. Vitals (BP, HR, SpO2) documented. IV site assessment.
DURING
IV site: location, gauge, number of attempts. Bag components and doses. Start time, flow rate. Patient tolerance during infusion.
AFTER
End time. Vitals post-infusion. Any adverse reactions. Site condition on removal. Patient education given. Follow-up plan.
BILLING
Retain copy of all orders. Document medical necessity for therapeutic drips. Cash-pay: itemized receipt for patient HSA/FSA reimbursement.
