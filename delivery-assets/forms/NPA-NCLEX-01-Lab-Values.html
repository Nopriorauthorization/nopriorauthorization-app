Section 01

Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP)

The BMP is an 8-test panel that gives a snapshot of kidney function, fluid and electrolyte balance, and blood glucose. It's ordered constantly — know every value cold.

🔢 Normal Ranges & Critical Values
Lab ValueNormal RangeCritical LowCritical HighClinical Note
Sodium (Na+)136–145 mEq/L<120>160Primary extracellular cation; water balance
Potassium (K+)3.5–5.0 mEq/L<2.5>6.5Cardiac conduction; muscle function
Chloride (Cl-)98–106 mEq/L<80>115Follows Na+; acid-base balance
CO2 / Bicarb (HCO3-)22–29 mEq/L<15>40Metabolic acid-base indicator
BUN10–20 mg/dL—>100Kidney function; protein catabolism
Creatinine0.6–1.2 mg/dL—>10Most specific kidney function marker
Glucose70–100 mg/dL<40>500Check fasting status; DM management
Calcium (Ca++)8.5–10.5 mg/dL<6>13Bone, muscle, nerve, cardiac function
💡
BUN:Creatinine Ratio
A ratio greater than 20:1 suggests prerenal failure (dehydration, decreased perfusion). A ratio less than 10:1 suggests intrinsic renal disease. This ratio is one of the most tested kidney concepts on NCLEX.

Section 02

Complete Metabolic Panel (CMP)

The CMP includes everything in the BMP plus liver function tests, total protein, and albumin. Think of it as the BMP + liver panel. Often ordered for a comprehensive baseline or to monitor chronic conditions.

⚠️
Low Albumin = Edema Risk
Albumin maintains oncotic pressure in the vessels. When albumin drops below 3.5 g/dL, fluid leaks out of the vasculature into tissues → edema. This is why malnourished or liver disease patients often present with swollen legs or ascites.

Section 03

Complete Blood Count (CBC)

The CBC gives a full picture of all three blood cell lines — red cells, white cells, and platelets. Abnormalities point to anemia, infection, bleeding disorders, and more.

📐
The H&H Rule
Hematocrit ≈ 3× Hemoglobin. If a patient's Hgb is 10, their Hct should be around 30%. If the values don't match up, suspect a lab error or specimen issue. This rule is a quick sanity check used in clinical practice daily.

🚨
Left Shift = Severe Infection
A "left shift" means immature neutrophils (band cells) are being released from the bone marrow into circulation — the body is working overtime to fight a serious infection. An ANC (absolute neutrophil count) below 500/μL = severe neutropenia → place patient in reverse/protective isolation.
🩺
Platelet <50,000 — Bleeding Precautions
When platelets drop below 50,000/μL, initiate bleeding precautions: no IM injections, no rectal temperatures, soft toothbrush, electric razor only, fall precautions. Below 20,000 = spontaneous bleeding risk. Below 10,000 = critical/life-threatening.

Section 04

Coagulation Studies

Coagulation labs are essential when patients are on anticoagulants or have bleeding disorders. Know the normal vs therapeutic ranges — they are different and both are tested.

🔢 Coagulation Reference
LabNormal RangeTherapeutic TargetKey Clinical Note
PT (Prothrombin Time)11–13.5 sec—Tests extrinsic pathway (Warfarin)
INR0.8–1.12.0–3.0 (Warfarin); 2.5–3.5 (mech valve)Standardized PT ratio — most used clinically
aPTT25–35 sec46–70 sec (Heparin therapy)Tests intrinsic pathway; monitor Heparin
Fibrinogen200–400 mg/dL—↓ = DIC, liver failure risk
D-Dimer<0.5 μg/mL—↑ = clot breakdown (DVT, PE, DIC)
💊
Warfarin Antidotes
Vitamin K — slow reversal (12–24 hrs). FFP (Fresh Frozen Plasma) — fast reversal (used in emergencies). INR >3.0 = hold dose + notify provider.
💉
Heparin Antidote
Protamine sulfate — given IV slowly. 1mg protamine neutralizes ~100 units of heparin. Watch for HIT (Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia) — paradoxical clotting.

Section 05

Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)

ABGs measure the balance between oxygen, carbon dioxide, and pH in arterial blood. They're used to evaluate respiratory and metabolic function — a cornerstone of critical care and high on the NCLEX.

🔢 ABG Normal Values
ComponentNormal RangeAcidosisAlkalosisControlled By
pH7.35–7.45<7.35>7.45Both lungs and kidneys
PaCO235–45 mmHg>45 (↑acid)<35 (↓acid)Lungs (respiratory)
HCO3-22–26 mEq/L<22 (↓base)>26 (↑base)Kidneys (metabolic)
PaO280–100 mmHg<60 = hypoxemiaLungs (gas exchange)
SaO295–100%<90% = critical hypoxiaOxygen saturation of Hgb
🔑
The ROME Method — Memorize This
Respiratory = Opposite: pH and CO2 move in opposite directions.
  ↓pH + ↑CO2 = Respiratory Acidosis  |  ↑pH + ↓CO2 = Respiratory Alkalosis

Metabolic = Equal: pH and HCO3- move in the same direction.
  ↓pH + ↓HCO3 = Metabolic Acidosis  |  ↑pH + ↑HCO3 = Metabolic Alkalosis

Cardiac Enzymes & Markers

Cardiac biomarkers are released into the bloodstream when heart muscle is damaged. Knowing which rises first, peaks highest, and stays longest is classic NCLEX material.

📊 Cardiac Marker Timeline
MarkerRisesPeaksReturns to NormalKey Point
Troponin I / T3–6 hrs12–24 hrs7–14 daysMost specific for MI; gold standard
CK-MB3–6 hrs12–24 hrs48–72 hrsCardiac-specific CK; used for reinfarction
Myoglobin1–3 hrs4–12 hrs24 hrsFirst to rise; not cardiac-specific
BNPHoursSustainedVaries with treatment>100 pg/mL = heart failure
💔
BNP in Heart Failure
BNP >100 pg/mL = heart failure. BNP >400 pg/mL = severe HF. BNP is released by the ventricles in response to increased wall stress — it's used to distinguish cardiac dyspnea from pulmonary dyspnea in the ER.

Section 07

Electrolyte Imbalances

Electrolyte imbalances are woven throughout NCLEX — they affect cardiac rhythm, muscle function, neuro status, and more. Know the symptoms and priority nursing actions for each.

Hypo-Na <136Hyponatremia: Headache, confusion, seizures, muscle cramps, nausea. Caused by SIADH, excess water intake, diuretics. Treat by restricting fluids; hypertonic saline only for severe symptomatic cases.
Hyper-Na >145Hypernatremia: Thirst, dry mucous membranes, agitation, tachycardia, decreased LOC. Caused by dehydration, diabetes insipidus. Treat with hypotonic fluids slowly.
Hypo-K <3.5Hypokalemia: Muscle weakness, fatigue, U wave on EKG, dysrhythmias, constipation. Caused by vomiting, diuretics, diarrhea. Give K+ slowly IV — never IV push. Always give with monitoring.
Hyper-K >5.0Hyperkalemia: Peaked T waves → wide QRS → sine wave pattern → VF/asystole. Weakness, nausea. Treat with calcium gluconate (stabilize heart), sodium bicarb, insulin + dextrose, Kayexalate, dialysis.
Hypo-Ca <8.5Hypocalcemia: Trousseau's sign (carpal spasm with BP cuff), Chvostek's sign (facial twitch with tap), tetany, seizures, prolonged QT. Treat with calcium gluconate IV.
Hyper-Ca >10.5Hypercalcemia: "Stones, bones, groans, moans" — kidney stones, bone pain, constipation, lethargy. Caused by hyperparathyroidism, cancer. Treat with IV fluids, furosemide, bisphosphonates.
Hypo-Mg <1.5Hypomagnesemia: Muscle cramps, tremors, irritability, prolonged QT, torsades de pointes risk. Caused by alcoholism, poor nutrition. Treat with Mag sulfate IV.

Section 08

High-Yield NCLEX Questions

These are the lab value scenarios that appear most frequently on the NCLEX. Read each question, think through your answer, then read the explanation carefully.

Q1A patient on Digoxin has a potassium level of 3.0 mEq/L. What is the priority nursing action?
Hold the Digoxin and notify the provider. Hypokalemia potentiates Digoxin toxicity — low K+ allows Digoxin to bind more aggressively to cardiac cells. Always check K+ before giving Digoxin. K+ must be within normal range before administering.
Q2A patient on Warfarin has an INR of 3.8. What is the nurse's first action?
Hold the dose and notify the provider. INR >3.0 on standard Warfarin therapy = supratherapeutic. Do NOT give Vitamin K without an order. Assess for signs of bleeding. Document and notify.
Q3A patient's platelet count is 38,000/μL. What precautions should the nurse initiate?
Bleeding precautions: No IM injections, no rectal temperatures, soft toothbrush, electric razor, fall precautions, avoid NSAIDs. Notify provider. Below 50,000 = high bleeding risk; below 20,000 = spontaneous bleeding risk.
Q4A patient's BUN is 48, creatinine is 1.1. What does this suggest and what is the priority intervention?
BUN:Creatinine ratio = 43:1 → Prerenal failure (dehydration). Creatinine is normal, suggesting kidneys are functioning but aren't receiving adequate perfusion. Priority = IV fluid resuscitation. Monitor urine output closely.
Q5A patient's hemoglobin is 6.8 g/dL. What should the nurse anticipate?
Expect a blood transfusion order. Hgb <7–8 g/dL typically triggers a transfusion in symptomatic patients. Ensure type and crossmatch is complete. Obtain IV access, assess for symptoms (fatigue, dyspnea, tachycardia), and monitor during transfusion.
Q6A patient reports chest pain. Troponin I is 0.09 ng/mL (normal <0.04). What does this indicate?
Elevated troponin = myocardial damage (MI). Troponin is the most specific cardiac marker. Peaks at 12–24 hours. Stays elevated for 7–14 days. Notify provider immediately, obtain 12-lead EKG, prepare for treatment protocol.
Q7A patient's EKG shows peaked T waves. What lab should the nurse check immediately?
Potassium (K+) — STAT. Peaked T waves are the earliest EKG sign of hyperkalemia. As K+ rises: peaked T waves → widened QRS → sine wave → VF/asystole. This is a cardiac emergency. Check K+, notify provider, prepare calcium gluconate.
Q8A patient with Type 1 DM has a blood glucose of 52 mg/dL and is alert and oriented. What is the priority?
Give 15g of fast-acting carbohydrates (Rule of 15). Orange juice, glucose tablets, regular soda. Wait 15 minutes, recheck glucose. Repeat if still <70. Once normalized, give a complex carb snack. Do NOT give insulin.
Q9A patient's WBC is 14,500 and they have a fever of 101.8°F. Blood cultures are pending. Should the nurse start antibiotics?
Yes — obtain cultures first, then start antibiotics as ordered. The sequence is: blood cultures × 2 → antibiotics. Do NOT delay antibiotics waiting for culture results once ordered. Each hour of delay in sepsis worsens outcomes.
Q10A patient has albumin of 2.8 g/dL and peripheral edema. How are these related?
Low albumin → decreased oncotic pressure → fluid moves into tissues → edema. Albumin holds fluid in the vascular space. When it drops, fluid leaks out. This is third-spacing. Common in malnutrition, liver disease, nephrotic syndrome.
