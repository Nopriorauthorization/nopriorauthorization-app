Frequently Asked Questions
Quick reference for patients
These are the 10 most common questions from patients starting peptide therapy. Your provider is always your best resource for protocol-specific questions.
How long until I see results?

Sleep improvement often comes within 2–4 weeks. Energy and body composition changes typically take 6–12 weeks. Full benefits build over 3–6 months of consistent use.

Do I need to inject every day?

Depends on your protocol. GH-releasing peptides like Ipamorelin/CJC are typically injected 5 days on, 2 days off. BPC-157 may be daily. Your provider will specify your schedule.

What if I miss a dose?

Skip it and resume your normal schedule. Do not double-dose. For GH-releasing peptides, one missed dose does not significantly affect results.

Does it hurt?

Insulin syringes are very thin — most patients rate discomfort at 1–2 out of 10. Allowing medication to reach room temperature before injecting reduces discomfort significantly.

Can I drink alcohol?

Occasional alcohol is generally acceptable. However, alcohol disrupts sleep quality and growth hormone release — which works against your goals. Moderation is strongly recommended.

Do I need to cycle off?

Many GH-releasing peptide protocols include breaks (e.g., 5 days on, 2 days off, or 3 months on, 1 month off). Your provider will advise on your specific protocol.

Can I use peptides with other medications?

Always disclose all medications to your provider. Most peptides have a favorable interaction profile, but some combinations require monitoring.

How should I store my medication?

Refrigerate reconstituted peptides. Do not freeze unless specifically instructed. Keep away from light. Most opened vials should be used within 28–30 days.

Will I gain weight?

GH-releasing peptides can cause temporary water retention early on. Over time, they support fat loss and lean muscle — not weight gain.

What if I have a reaction?

Mild injection site reactions are common. Contact Hello Gorgeous Med Spa at 630-636-6193 if you experience spreading redness, fever, or significant swelling.
