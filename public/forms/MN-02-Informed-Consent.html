Collagen Induction Therapy
Procedure Description

Microneedling, also known as Collagen Induction Therapy (CIT), is a minimally invasive cosmetic procedure that uses a sterile device containing fine needles to create controlled micro-injuries in the skin's surface. These micro-channels stimulate the body's natural wound-healing response, triggering the production of new collagen and elastin fibers. The procedure may be performed with topical serums (such as hyaluronic acid or growth factors) to enhance penetration and results. Treatment depth, speed, and technique are customized based on your skin type, area of concern, and desired outcome.

Expected Outcomes

Most patients notice improved skin texture, tone, and firmness within 2–4 weeks of treatment, with optimal results developing over 3–6 months as new collagen matures. A series of 3–6 treatments spaced 4–6 weeks apart is typically recommended for best results. Individual outcomes vary based on age, skin condition, lifestyle factors, and adherence to pre- and post-care instructions.

Risks & Complications
Common (expected)Redness lasting 24–72 hours, mild swelling, pinpoint bleeding during treatment, skin tightness and warmth, temporary sensitivity
Less CommonBruising, prolonged redness beyond 72 hours, acne breakouts or purging in the first week, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (especially Fitzpatrick IV–VI), peeling or flaking
RareInfection (bacterial, viral, or fungal), scarring (hypertrophic or atrophic), herpes simplex reactivation, allergic reaction to topical serum, prolonged changes in skin pigmentation, persistent erythema

Treatment Plan
Standard protocol: 3–6 sessions spaced 4–6 weeks apart. Deeper scars may require additional sessions at greater depths.
Treatment Alternatives

Alternatives to microneedling include, but are not limited to: chemical peels, fractional laser resurfacing, platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy, radiofrequency microneedling, dermabrasion, topical retinoid therapy, and dermal fillers. Each alternative carries its own risks and benefits. Your provider can discuss which options may be appropriate for your specific concerns.
