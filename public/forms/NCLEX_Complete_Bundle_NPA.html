⚡ Electrolyte Imbalance Quick ID
HypoNa
Low sodium <136: headache, confusion, seizures, muscle cramps. SIADH common cause.
HyperNa
High sodium >145: thirst, dry mucous membranes, agitation, tachycardia. Dehydration.
HypoK
Low K+ <3.5: muscle weakness, U wave on EKG, dysrhythmias. Give K+ slowly IV.
HyperK
High K+ >5.0: peaked T waves, widened QRS, bradycardia → cardiac arrest.
HypoCa
Low Ca++ <8.5: Trousseau's / Chvostek's sign, tetany, seizures.
HyperCa
High Ca++ >10.5: "Stones, bones, groans, moans" — kidney stones, bone pain, constipation.

🔡 Suffix → Drug Class Decoder
-olol — Beta Blockers
metoprolol, atenolol, carvedilol → HR↓, BP↓. Check HR before giving (<60 = hold)
-pril — ACE Inhibitors
lisinopril, enalapril, captopril → "prils cause dry cough." SE: hyperkalemia, angioedema
-sartan — ARBs
losartan, valsartan → similar to ACEi, NO cough. SE: hyperkalemia, renal impairment
-statin — Statins (HMG-CoA reductase)
atorvastatin, simvastatin → take at night. SE: myopathy, rhabdomyolysis (check CK)
-dipine — Calcium Channel Blockers
amlodipine, nifedipine → BP↓, angina. Avoid grapefruit. SE: flushing, edema
-mycin / -cin — Antibiotics
erythromycin, vancomycin → check "Red Man Syndrome" with vanco. Monitor troughs.
-floxacin — Fluoroquinolones
ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin → tendon rupture risk, avoid in <18. Sun sensitivity.
-mab — Monoclonal Antibodies
rituximab, trastuzumab → biologic/immunotherapy. Infusion rxn risk. Monitor closely.
-tidine — H2 Blockers
famotidine, ranitidine → reduce gastric acid. Less potent than PPIs.
-prazole — PPIs
omeprazole, pantoprazole → strongest acid suppression. Take 30 min before meals.
⚠️ High-Alert Medications (ISMP List)
1
Insulin — always double-check dose with another RN. Never abbreviate "U" (looks like 0). Use insulin syringe only.
2
Heparin — weight-based dosing. Monitor aPTT. Antidote: protamine sulfate. Risk: HIT (check platelets).
3
Warfarin — monitor INR. Many drug/food interactions. Antidote: Vitamin K. Bleeding risk.
4
Opioids — respiratory depression. Have Naloxone (Narcan) available. Assess RR before giving (<12 = hold).
5
Digoxin — narrow therapeutic index (0.5–2 ng/mL). Check pulse before giving (<60 = hold). K+ must be normal.
6
Concentrated electrolytes (KCl, NaCl) — never give IV push. Always dilute. Must be on restricted access list.
7
Chemotherapy — double-check drug, dose, route, patient. PPE required. Extravasation = immediate action.
8
Neuromuscular blocking agents — patient awake but paralyzed. Must have ventilator ready. Never use without sedation.
ALWAYS check: 6 rights + 2 patient identifiers before ANY high-alert med.

🩺 Nursing Considerations by Drug Class
BB
Beta Blockers: Hold if HR <60 or SBP <90. Never stop abruptly (rebound HTN). Monitor glucose (masks hypoglycemia signs).
DU
Diuretics (loop/thiazide): Monitor K+, weight, I&O. Furosemide = "furious urination." Give early AM. Ototoxicity with high doses.
IN
Insulin: Glargine (Lantus) = NO peak, once daily. Regular = short-acting, only clear insulin given IV. Lispro = rapid-acting, give WITH meal.
AC
ACE Inhibitors: Dry cough = common SE. Switch to ARB if intolerable. Do NOT use in pregnancy (teratogenic).
DG
Digoxin: Therapeutic: 0.5–2 ng/mL. Toxicity signs: nausea, visual changes (yellow-green halos), bradycardia.
AB
Aminoglycosides (gentamicin): Monitor peak & trough levels. Nephrotoxic + ototoxic. Avoid with other nephrotoxic drugs.
SS
Steroids: Immunosuppressive. Monitor blood glucose (↑), infections (mask fever). Do not stop abruptly. Moon face, buffalo hump = Cushingoid.
LI
Lithium: Narrow therapeutic index (0.6–1.2 mEq/L). Toxicity: tremor, confusion, polyuria. Na+ intake must be consistent.
PH
Phenytoin: Monitor levels (10–20 mcg/mL). Gingival hyperplasia, teratogenic. Always infuse slowly with NS (no dextrose).

💓 Step-by-Step Strip Interpretation
1
Rate: Is it 60–100? Bradycardia (<60) or tachycardia (>100)?
2
Rhythm: Regular or irregular? Measure R-R intervals. Consistently same = regular.
3
P waves: Present? Upright? One P before every QRS? Same shape each time?
4
PR interval: 0.12–0.20 sec? Consistent? Lengthening = heart block.
5
QRS: <0.12 sec = narrow (supraventricular). >0.12 sec = wide (ventricular origin).
6
ST segment: Elevated (>1mm) = STEMI. Depressed = ischemia/NSTEMI.
7
Name it: Identify rhythm, correlate with patient clinical picture.
Key rule: Always treat the PATIENT, not the monitor. Assess symptoms first.
💊 EKG Changes from Electrolytes
HypoK
Flat/inverted T waves, prominent U wave, ST depression → dysrhythmias
HyperK
Peaked T waves → wide QRS → sine wave pattern → VF/asystole
HypoCa
Prolonged QT interval → torsades de pointes risk
HyperCa
Shortened QT, bradycardia, heart block
HypoMg
Prolonged QT, torsades de pointes (treat with Mag sulfate)
🚨 Life-Threatening Rhythms — Act NOW
Ventricular Fibrillation (VF)
No identifiable waves — chaotic. No pulse. → DEFIBRILLATE immediately. CPR. ACLS.
Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)
Wide QRS (>0.12), rate 100–250. WITH pulse → cardiovert or amiodarone. WITHOUT pulse → treat as VF.
Asystole ("Flatline")
No electrical activity. → CPR + epinephrine. Check lead placement first. Not shockable.
Torsades de Pointes
Twisting QRS around baseline. Long QT. Treat with Magnesium sulfate IV. If pulseless → defib.
3rd Degree (Complete) Heart Block
P waves and QRS independent — no relationship. Bradycardia. → Atropine, pacemaker.
Pulseless rhythms: VF, VT(pulseless), asystole, PEA → CPR + ACLS
📋 Common Non-Lethal Rhythms
Normal Sinus Rhythm (NSR)
Rate 60–100, regular, P before every QRS, PR 0.12–0.20, QRS <0.12. Goal rhythm.
Sinus Bradycardia
Rate <60, otherwise normal. Treat only if symptomatic. Atropine first line. Pacemaker if refractory.
Sinus Tachycardia
Rate >100, normal P/QRS. Treat the CAUSE (fever, pain, hypovolemia, anxiety).
Atrial Fibrillation (A-Fib)
No P waves, irregularly irregular. Rate control (beta blockers, digoxin). Anticoagulate (clot risk!).
Atrial Flutter
Sawtooth P waves, regular. Rate ~300 atrial/150 ventricular. Cardiovert or rate control.
PVCs (Premature Ventricular Contractions)
Early, wide QRS, no P wave. Occasional = benign. Frequent (>6/min) or consecutive = concerning.

⚠️ HIGH-YIELD NCLEX EKG QUESTIONS
Q1
Patient on monitor shows VF → first action = DEFIBRILLATE (unsynchronized). Not cardiovert.
Q2
A-Fib patient — why anticoagulate? → Clot forms in atria (no contraction) → embolize → stroke.
Q3
Peaked T waves on monitor → check K+ STAT. Hyperkalemia = cardiac emergency.
Q4
Difference between cardioversion and defibrillation? → Cardioversion = synchronized (has pulse). Defib = unsynchronized (pulseless).
Q5
Patient in VT with a pulse → amiodarone or synchronized cardioversion. Stable vs unstable determines approach.
Q6
ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF → inferior MI (RCA). Leads V1–V4 = anterior MI (LAD).
Q7
First drug for symptomatic bradycardia? → Atropine 0.5mg IV. If refractory → transcutaneous pacing.
Q8
Torsades de pointes → treat with Magnesium sulfate. Know the twisted ribbon appearance.
Q9
Digoxin toxicity EKG → bradycardia + "scooped" ST depression. Check dig level + K+.
Q10
Patient with new A-Fib >48 hours — before cardioversion → rule out atrial thrombus (TEE or 3 weeks anticoag).

🫁 Respiratory Acidosis
pH
pH <7.35 + CO2 >45 mmHg
WHY
CO2 is an acid. ↑CO2 = ↑acid = ↓pH. Lungs aren't blowing off CO2 (hypoventilation).
Causes:
C
COPD, pneumonia, asthma, pneumothorax, airway obstruction
C
CNS depression (opioids, head injury, anesthesia) → ↓ respiratory drive
C
Neuromuscular disease (Guillain-Barré, myasthenia gravis) → ↓ breathing effort
S&S:
Headache, confusion, lethargy, cyanosis, ↑HR, ↑RR (compensating), CO2 narcosis in COPD
Compensation: Kidneys retain HCO3- (takes hours to days). Treatment: Improve ventilation, bronchodilators, positioning, O2 (careful in COPD).
🌬️ Respiratory Alkalosis
pH
pH >7.45 + CO2 <35 mmHg
WHY
Hyperventilation blows off too much CO2 → not enough acid → pH rises.
Causes:
C
Anxiety, panic attack, hyperventilation, pain
C
Fever, sepsis (early), pregnancy, high altitude
C
Mechanical ventilation (rate/tidal volume too high)
S&S:
Lightheadedness, tingling (perioral/fingertips), tetany, tachycardia, anxiety
Treatment: Slow breathing, treat cause, breathe into paper bag (anxiety). Comp: Kidneys excrete HCO3-.
🍋 Metabolic Acidosis
pH
pH <7.35 + HCO3- <22 mEq/L
WHY
Too much acid or too little bicarb. Body losing base or gaining acid.
C
DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis), lactic acidosis, renal failure
C
Diarrhea (loss of HCO3-), aspirin OD, methanol/ethylene glycol
S&S: Kussmaul respirations (deep, rapid = compensating), fruity breath (DKA), lethargy, nausea, cardiac dysrhythmias
Compensation: Lungs hyperventilate to blow off CO2 (fast). Tx: Treat cause. DKA → insulin + fluids. Bicarb if severe.
💊 Metabolic Alkalosis
pH
pH >7.45 + HCO3- >26 mEq/L
WHY
Too much base or loss of acid. Common in vomiting, NG suction.
C
Prolonged vomiting/NG suctioning (lose HCl acid)
C
Excessive antacid use, loop/thiazide diuretics (lose K+ → bicarb retained)
C
Hypokalemia (K+ shifts to compensate, H+ excreted)
S&S: Confusion, muscle weakness, cramping, tetany, hypoventilation (compensating)
Compensation: Lungs hypoventilate (retain CO2). Tx: Replace K+, Cl-. Treat cause. Stop offending agent.
⚠️ HIGH-YIELD NCLEX ABG QUESTIONS
Q1
pH 7.28, CO2 58, HCO3 26 → Respiratory Acidosis, uncompensated (HCO3 still normal).
Q2
pH 7.48, CO2 30, HCO3 22 → Respiratory Alkalosis, uncompensated. Think anxiety/hyperventilation.
Q3
pH 7.30, CO2 40, HCO3 18 → Metabolic Acidosis, uncompensated. Think DKA, renal failure, diarrhea.
Q4
pH 7.50, CO2 45, HCO3 32 → Metabolic Alkalosis, uncompensated. Think vomiting, NG suction.
Q5
COPD patient → use controlled O2 (1–2L NC). High O2 removes hypoxic drive → respiratory failure.
Q6
DKA patient breathing deeply and rapidly → Kussmaul respirations = metabolic acidosis compensation.
Q7
pH 7.38, CO2 50, HCO3 30 → Fully compensated Respiratory Acidosis. pH normal; CO2 and HCO3 both abnormal.
Q8
Patient on NG suction develops tetany → Metabolic Alkalosis from H+ loss via NG drainage.

🔄 Compensation Rules
RA
Resp Acidosis → Kidneys retain HCO3- (days) → HCO3 rises toward normal
RAlk
Resp Alkalosis → Kidneys excrete HCO3- (days) → HCO3 drops toward normal
MA
Met Acidosis → Lungs hyperventilate, blow off CO2 (minutes) → CO2 drops toward normal
MAlk
Met Alkalosis → Lungs hypoventilate, retain CO2 (minutes) → CO2 rises toward normal
Respiratory compensation: Minutes–hours. Metabolic compensation: Hours–days. When pH normalizes = FULLY compensated.
🔍 Clinical Clues by Cause
DKA
Fruity breath, Kussmaul resp, high glucose, Met Acidosis
COPD
Barrel chest, pursed lips, chronic CO2 retention, Resp Acidosis (chronic)
V/NG
Vomiting or NG suction → lose HCl → Met Alkalosis + hypoK
ANX
Anxiety/panic attack → hyperventilate → Resp Alkalosis → tingling
ARF
Acute renal failure → can't excrete acid → Met Acidosis
DIV
Diarrhea → lose HCO3- → Met Acidosis (opposite of vomiting!)

1ST

1ST

1ST

1ST

1ST

LAST
Patient requesting sleeping pill, requesting pain med who rates pain 4/10 and is sleeping
👥 Delegation: RN vs LPN vs CNA
RN Only
• Assessment / initial assessment
• Nursing diagnosis
• Plan of care development
• IV push medications
• Blood transfusions
• Patient/family education
• Evaluation of outcomes
• Unstable patients
• Complex wound care
• Central line care/dressing

• Data collection (not full assessment)
• Routine medications (PO, SubQ, IM)
• Wound care (stable/routine)
• Urinary catheter insertion
• Nasogastric tube (after RN places)
• IV fluids (after RN initiates)
• Reinforce patient teaching
• Stable chronic patients
CNA / UAP — STABLE patients ONLY:
Vital signs (routine), bathing, grooming, ambulation, repositioning, feeding (no swallowing issues), I&O, urine specimens, non-sterile linen changes, weighing patient

NO
Any assessment or evaluation of patient status
NO

NO
Teaching or discharge education
NO
Invasive procedures (catheter insertion, wound care)
NO
Unstable patients or those with changing conditions
NO
Patients with swallowing difficulty (feeding risk)
NO
Blood transfusion monitoring or IV care

🧠 NGN: Clinical Judgment Model
1
Recognize cues — what data is relevant? What's abnormal?
2
Analyze cues — what do they mean together? What's the clinical picture?
3
Prioritize hypotheses — what's the most likely problem? Most urgent?
4
Generate solutions — what nursing actions address the priority problem?
5
Take action — implement the highest priority intervention first
6
Evaluate outcomes — did it work? What's next?
This is the Next Gen NCLEX framework. Every NGN item tests this process. Think it through even when you're not sure.
⚠️ HIGH-YIELD PRIORITY QUESTIONS
Q1
Four patients need attention — who is first? → The one with airway/breathing compromise.
Q2
Can you delegate VS to the CNA for a post-op patient? → Yes, if stable. RN still evaluates the results.
Q3
LPN asks to do discharge teaching → No. Teaching = RN only. LPN can reinforce, not initiate.
Q4
Patient just returned from surgery — who sees them first? → Newly returned post-op = highest priority.
Q5
Psychosocial need vs. physiological need → Physiological wins every time on NCLEX.
📝 5 Rights of Delegation
1
Right Task — is this task appropriate to delegate? Not requiring RN judgment?
2
Right Circumstance — is the patient stable? Is the situation appropriate?
3
Right Person — does this person have the training/license to do this task?
4
Right Direction — are instructions clear, specific, and understood?
5
Right Supervision — will the RN monitor, follow up, and evaluate outcomes?
🔄 Assignment vs Delegation
Assignment
Distributing work to those with the scope and skill to do it. Charge nurse to staff RN = assignment, not delegation.
Delegation
RN transfers a specific task to a person who would not otherwise do it. Requires the 5 rights. RN remains accountable.

NCLEX Infection Control & Isolation Precautions

No Prior Authorization · Danielle Alcala-Glazier, RN Student · Licensed Esthetician · 10+ Years Clinical Practice
NPA
SIDE 1
🛡️ Standard Precautions (ALL Patients)
Used with every patient, every time — regardless of diagnosis.
HH
Hand hygiene — most important infection control measure. Before and after every patient contact.
GL
Gloves — when touching blood, body fluids, mucous membranes, non-intact skin
GN
Gown — if clothing may be contaminated with blood or body fluids
MK
Mask/eye protection — when splashing of blood/body fluids is possible
SP
Safe injection practices — never recap needles, use safety devices
RE
Respiratory hygiene/cough etiquette — masks for coughing patients in waiting areas
Hand hygiene: Soap + water (30 sec minimum) OR hand sanitizer — use soap for C. diff and norovirus (alcohol doesn't kill spores).
💧 Contact Precautions
PPE
Gown + Gloves — required upon entering room
Rm
Private room or cohort with same illness
Diseases requiring CONTACT precautions:
Cdiff
C. difficile — Use soap & water (NOT hand sanitizer). Spore-forming.
MRSA
MRSA (skin/wound) — Methicillin-resistant Staph aureus
VRE
VRE — Vancomycin-resistant Enterococcus
RSV
RSV — respiratory syncytial virus (especially infants)
HSV
Herpes simplex (disseminated), impetigo, scabies, wound infections
Noro
Norovirus — soap & water only. Highly contagious. GI spread.
Key: Dedicated equipment per patient. Don't share stethoscopes, BP cuffs. Discard PPE before leaving room.
💨 Droplet Precautions
PPE
Surgical Mask (standard, not N95) — worn within 3 feet of patient
Rm
Private room preferred. Keep door closed. Patient wears mask during transport.
Diseases requiring DROPLET precautions:
Flu
Influenza — seasonal and pandemic strains
Strp
Strep pharyngitis, scarlet fever, Group A Strep
Men
Meningitis (bacterial — Neisseria meningitidis)
Pert
Pertussis (whooping cough)
Rub
Rubella (German measles), Mumps
DPH
Diphtheria (pharyngeal)
Adeno
Adenovirus, Parvovirus B19
Droplet vs airborne: Droplets fall >3 feet (large). Airborne particles stay suspended (tiny). This changes PPE and room type.
🌬️ Airborne Precautions
PPE
N95 respirator (fitted/tested) — not a surgical mask. Must seal to face.
Rm
Negative pressure room — door stays CLOSED. Air exhausted outside or HEPA filtered.
Diseases requiring AIRBORNE precautions:
TB
Tuberculosis — most tested. Negative pressure room is #1 priority. Patient wears surgical mask outside room.
Meas
Measles (Rubeola) — highly contagious airborne virus. Koplik spots on buccal mucosa.
Chkpx
Chickenpox (Varicella) AND Shingles (Zoster, disseminated) — also add contact precautions.
SARS
SARS-CoV-2 / COVID-19 — N95 for aerosol-generating procedures at minimum
Mnemonic: MTV — Measles, TB, Varicella/Zoster = Airborne
🧤 PPE Donning & Doffing Order
DONNING (Putting ON):
Gown
→
Mask
→
Goggles/Shield
→
Gloves
Gloves go on LAST because they cover the gown cuffs
DOFFING (Taking OFF):
Gloves
→
Goggles
→
Gown
→
Mask
Mask comes off LAST (most contaminated on outside = face protection). Perform hand hygiene after every step.
Most contaminated PPE when removing: Gloves > gown front > face shield > mask ties. Touch only clean inner surfaces.

Q1

Q2

🔬 Chain of Infection
1
Infectious Agent — bacteria, virus, fungus, parasite
2
Reservoir — where pathogen lives (person, animal, environment)
3
Portal of Exit — how pathogen leaves host (respiratory, GI, skin)
4
Mode of Transmission — contact, droplet, airborne, vector
5
Portal of Entry — how it enters new host (respiratory, GI, broken skin)
6
Susceptible Host — immunocompromised, elderly, very young, malnourished
Break any link → prevent infection. Hand hygiene breaks transmission. PPE breaks portal of entry. Vaccines reduce susceptibility.

SIDE 1
🤰 Stages of Labor
S1
Stage 1 — Dilation: Onset of contractions to full dilation (10 cm)
• Latent: 0–3 cm; mild, irregular contractions; patient talkative
• Active: 4–7 cm; moderate contractions q3–5 min; needs coaching
• Transition: 8–10 cm; intense, q2–3 min; most painful phase
S2
Stage 2 — Pushing: 10 cm to delivery of baby. Average: primip 2 hr, multip 30 min.
S3
Stage 3 — Placenta: Delivery of placenta. Usually within 5–30 min. Check for intact placenta.
S4
Stage 4 — Recovery: First 1–4 hrs postpartum. Highest risk for hemorrhage. Assess q15 min.
Assess during labor: FHR, contraction frequency/duration/intensity, cervical dilation, fetal station, membrane status.
💓 Fetal Heart Rate — Interpretation
PatternTypeMeaning & Action
FHR 110–160NormalReassuring. Continue monitoring.
Acceleration (+15 bpm × 15 sec)ReassuringFetal well-being. Good sign.
Early DecelBenignHead compression (mirrors contraction). No intervention.
Variable DecelConcerningCord compression. Reposition, O2, fluids. If persistent → notify.
Late DecelNon-reassuringUteroplacental insufficiency. Reposition, O2, stop Pitocin, call provider.
Prolonged Decel >2 minEmergencyReposition, O2, call OB, prepare for delivery.
Late decels = ALWAYS non-reassuring. Indicates baby not getting enough O2 from placenta. Priority action: reposition mother to left lateral, give O2, stop Pitocin.
🚨 OB Emergencies — Act Immediately
PPH
Postpartum Hemorrhage: Blood loss >500 mL (vaginal) or >1000 mL (C-section). Uterine atony most common cause. → Fundal massage, oxytocin, bimanual compression, notify OB.
PRE
Preeclampsia: BP ≥140/90 after 20 wks + proteinuria. Severe: BP ≥160/110, headache, visual changes, RUQ pain, seizures. → Magnesium sulfate (seizure prevention), antihypertensives.
EC
Eclampsia = preeclampsia + seizures. Priority: Magnesium sulfate + seizure precautions + O2. Have calcium gluconate at bedside (mag antidote).
AP
Abruptio Placentae: Placenta separates before delivery. Painful, rigid abdomen, vaginal bleeding. Emergency C-section possible.
PP
Placenta Previa: Placenta covers cervical os. Painless bright red vaginal bleeding. No vaginal exams. Bed rest, C-section.
CP
Cord Prolapse: Cord exits before baby. Relieve compression — knee-chest or Trendelenburg, push up presenting part, emergency C-section.
SE
Shoulder Dystocia: Anterior shoulder stuck behind pubic symphysis. McRoberts maneuver (hyperflex knees), suprapubic pressure.
💊 Pitocin (Oxytocin) Nursing Care
USE
Induce or augment labor. Also used postpartum to contract uterus and prevent hemorrhage.
STOP
STOP Pitocin if: Contractions >90 sec or <2 min apart; non-reassuring FHR (late decels); uterine hyperstimulation.
MON
Monitor FHR continuously. Monitor contractions frequency, duration, intensity. Check BP frequently.
Postpartum use: 10–40 units/L IV after placenta delivery → uterine contraction → reduces hemorrhage risk.

NCLEX Mental Health & Therapeutic Communication

No Prior Authorization · Danielle Alcala-Glazier, RN Student · Licensed Esthetician · 10+ Years Clinical Practice
NPA
SIDE 1
💬 Therapeutic Communication
DO USE — Therapeutic Techniques:
✓
Open-ended questions: "Tell me more about what you're feeling."
✓
Reflection: "It sounds like you're feeling overwhelmed." (mirror feelings)
✓
Clarification: "I'm not sure I understand — can you explain that more?"
✓
Restating: Repeat key words to show you heard. "You said you feel alone."
✓
Silence: Allow pauses. Don't fill every gap. Silence = presence.
✓
Focusing: "Let's talk more about that." Helps narrow the conversation.
✓
General leads: "Go on…" "Tell me about that." Encourages the patient to continue.
AVOID — Non-Therapeutic Responses:
✗
False reassurance: "Everything will be fine." "Don't worry."
✗
Giving advice: "You should leave him." "You need to exercise."
✗
Changing the subject: Dismisses the patient's feelings.
✗
Closed-ended questions: "Are you feeling better?" (yes/no only)
✗
Why questions: "Why did you do that?" — sounds judgmental.
✗
Approving/disapproving: "That's great!" or "That's wrong." Imposes values.
🚨 Safety Priority: Suicide & Self-Harm
HIGH
High-risk factors: Specific plan + means + intent, prior attempts, hopelessness, male gender, elderly, access to firearms, recent loss
SAF
Safety first: Assess, remove hazards from room (sharps, cords, call light cords, belts). 1:1 observation if high risk.
ASK
Ask directly: "Are you thinking about hurting yourself?" — Asking does NOT increase risk. Research confirms this.
CON
Do NOT leave patient alone if expressing suicidal ideation. Do NOT make promises to keep secrets.
NO-NO
Never promise confidentiality about safety concerns. "I can't keep that secret, but I can help you."
Priority nursing action: Patient safety ALWAYS before therapeutic communication when safety is at risk.
🧠 Psychiatric Disorders — Quick Reference
SZ
Schizophrenia: Positive sx (hallucinations, delusions, disorganized speech) + negative sx (flat affect, alogia, avolition). Do NOT argue with delusions.
BD
Bipolar Disorder: Manic phase (grandiosity, impulsivity, decreased sleep, flight of ideas) + depressive phase. Lithium = mood stabilizer.
MDD
Major Depression: Depressed mood, anhedonia, sleep/appetite changes, psychomotor changes, suicidal ideation. SSRIs are first line.
ANX
Anxiety/Panic Disorder: Palpitations, sweating, chest pain, fear of dying. Remain calm with patient. Slow breathing. Benzos short-term.
PTSD
PTSD: Flashbacks, hypervigilance, avoidance, nightmares. Trauma-informed care. No sudden movements or touch without warning.
OCD
OCD: Obsessions + compulsions to reduce anxiety. Do not interrupt rituals abruptly. SSRIs + CBT.
BPD
Borderline PD: Unstable relationships, identity, mood; splitting; self-harm. Set clear limits. Consistent approach across staff.

⚠️ HIGH-YIELD NCLEX MENTAL HEALTH QUESTIONS
Q1
Patient says "the nurses are poisoning my food" — best response? → "I can eat with you to show it's safe" or acknowledge feelings. Do NOT argue with delusion.
Q2
Patient on MAOI wants to eat a salami sandwich — nurse's action? → Teach to avoid tyramine foods. Risk of hypertensive crisis.
Q3
Lithium level = 2.5 mEq/L — action? → Hold medication, notify prescriber immediately. Toxic level (>1.5 = toxic; >2.0 = severe).
Q4
Patient with schizophrenia stops antipsychotic — greatest risk? → Relapse of psychosis. Teach medication adherence. Depot injections for non-adherence.
Q5
Patient in manic phase — priority? → Safety first. High risk for injury (impulsivity), poor nutrition, sleep deprivation.
Q6
New SSRI started — when will patient feel better? → 2–4 weeks. Educate patient to continue taking it even if they don't feel different right away.
Q7
Patient discloses suicidal plan to nurse — nurse's obligation? → Break confidentiality to maintain safety. Report to treatment team immediately.
Q8
Therapeutic response to "I'm worthless" → "Tell me more about those feelings." NOT: "Don't say that" or "You're not worthless."
Q9
Involuntary commitment — when is it legal? → Imminent danger to self OR others + unable to care for self due to mental illness.
Q10
Tardive dyskinesia — cause? → Long-term typical antipsychotics. Irreversible. May require medication change.
