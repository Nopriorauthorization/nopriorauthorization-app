Cannula vs Needle — At a Glance
Cannula (Blunt Tip)
Flexible, blunt-tipped — pushes vessels aside
Single entry point — multiple directions from one hole
Significantly reduced bruising and swelling
Cannot penetrate vessel walls as easily
Requires larger entry needle (1 gauge up)
Slower technique — less precise placement for small deposits
Best for: large volume, high-risk vascular zones, linear threading
Needle (Sharp Tip)
Sharp — can penetrate vessels if not careful
Each deposit = new puncture site
Higher bruising and swelling risk
Can precisely target small anatomical structures
Direct access to target without prep needle
Faster for multi-point small deposit techniques
Best for: precise bolus, small deposits, structural supraperiosteal

Technique Tips — Cannula
Entry holeCreate entry with a sharp needle 1 gauge larger than cannula. Angle 45°, pop through dermis, then go horizontal
DirectionAlways move cannula before injecting — inject while withdrawing (retrograde) not advancing
Feel for resistanceResistance = tissue plane change. Reposition before injecting. Never force the cannula
No blood = saferBlood in hub = intravascular — withdraw, apply pressure, reassess. Do not inject
Less is moreDeposit less volume per pass — you can always add. Overcorrection with cannula is harder to fix than with needle
Aspiration — Does It Prevent Vascular Occlusion?
⚠️ Aspiration does NOT reliably prevent intravascular injection. Vessels are compressible — negative aspiration does NOT confirm extravascular placement.
✓ Aspiration is ONE tool in your safety toolkit — not a guarantee.
✓ Your true protection: anatomical knowledge + slow injection + small volumes + cannula in high-risk zones + hyaluronidase always in room.
★ If you see blood in the hub — stop. Apply pressure. Reassess before continuing.
