Section 01
Skin Analysis & Assessment
The foundation of every treatment — how to see the skin correctly before you touch it
From Danielle
"The consultation is the treatment. Everything that happens before a provider touches the skin determines whether the outcome is excellent or mediocre. Slow down. Ask the questions. Look at the skin in good lighting. Every great result starts here."
The Consultation Environment
Lighting: Bright, even, natural-simulating light. Magnification lamp for close assessment. Never assess skin in dim or warm-tinted light — it masks concerns.
No makeup: Client should arrive with clean skin or allow time for makeup removal before analysis. You cannot accurately assess skin through foundation.
Woods Lamp (optional): UV light device that reveals dehydration (purple/violet), oiliness (orange/yellow fluorescence), pigmentation (brown spots appear darker), bacteria (coral/pink fluorescence).
Skin scope / analyzer: Digital skin analysis devices provide objective data on pore size, wrinkle depth, moisture, and melanin. Excellent for patient education and tracking progress.

The 8 Consultation Questions — Ask Every New Client
"What brings you in today? What bothers you most?" — Primary concern in their words, not yours.
"Walk me through your morning and evening skincare routine." — Reveals products, habits, possible irritants.
"Are you using sunscreen daily? Which one?" — SPF compliance is the single most important skincare behavior.
"Any prescription skincare or oral medications?" — Accutane, retinoids, antibiotics, hormonal contraception all affect treatment.
"Have you ever reacted to a skincare product or treatment?" — Identifies sensitivities and contraindications early.
"Any facials, peels, laser, or injectables in the past 4 weeks?" — Recent treatments change what is safe today.
"Is there a specific event or timeline you're working toward?" — Sets realistic expectations and treatment planning.
"How much sun exposure do you get? Any recent tan or self-tanner?" — Critical for any laser, IPL, or chemical exfoliation service.

The Injectable Overlay — What Estheticians Must Know

Why estheticians need to know injectable anatomy: You will treat patients who have had recent Botox and filler. Understanding where products are placed changes how you perform facial massage, microneedling, heat treatments, and chemical services. Treating the wrong area the wrong way can migrate filler, reduce Botox duration, or cause complications.

Section 03
The Step-by-Step Facial Protocol
Complete 60-minute treatment protocol — every step, technique, timing, and rationale
From Danielle
"A great facial is not a menu of steps performed in order. It's a clinical conversation with the skin. The protocol is your framework — your observation, your judgment, and your technique are what make it a result."
Pre-Treatment Setup (10 minutes before client arrives)
Fresh linens on treatment table. Clean head rest cover. Blanket available.
Products staged in order of use — cleanser, toner, enzyme/exfoliant, ampoules, mask, moisturizer, SPF.
Towels or warm towel cabbie heated and ready. Facial steamer (if using) warmed 10 minutes prior.
Magnification lamp positioned and on. Tools (extractors, brushes) sanitized and covered.
Client chart and intake form reviewed. Note any contraindications, recent treatments, or product sensitivities.
The 60-Minute Classic Facial — Step by Step
1
Minutes 0–5

Consultation & Visual Analysis

Client reclined. Review intake form. Ask the 8 consultation questions. Perform visual skin analysis under magnification lamp. Assess skin type, concerns, contraindications. Explain your findings and the treatment plan. Obtain verbal consent for any modifications.

2
Minutes 5–12

Double Cleanse

First cleanse — makeup and surface removal: Apply cleansing oil or micellar balm with dry hands to dry face. Massage gently using circular motions to emulsify makeup, SPF, and surface impurities. Remove with warm damp sponges or muslin cloth. Second cleanse — skin cleanse: Apply appropriate cleanser for skin type. Massage 60 seconds using effleurage technique. Remove thoroughly with warm water and sponges. Pat dry. Skin should be clean but not stripped.

3
Minutes 12–17

Tone & Prep

Apply appropriate toner to cotton pad or hands. Toner serves three purposes: (1) remove any remaining cleanser residue, (2) restore pH balance, (3) prepare skin for active ingredients. For hydrating toner — press into skin. For exfoliating toner (AHA/BHA) — swipe gently, follow instructions for that specific product. Allow to absorb fully before next step.

4
Minutes 17–25

Exfoliation (choose one method per service)

Enzyme treatment: Apply warm enzyme mask to face avoiding eye area. Enzyme digests dead skin protein (keratin) through proteolytic action. Leave 5–10 minutes depending on sensitivity. Remove with warm damp sponges. Gentle enough for sensitive skin, rosacea, and Fitzpatrick V–VI.
Chemical exfoliation (peel): See Section 06 — Chemical Exfoliation Reference for specific protocols by acid type and depth.
Dermaplaning: Hold skin taut. Blade at 45° angle. Short, feathered strokes in direction of hair growth. Removes vellus hair and dead skin simultaneously. Not for active acne, rosacea, or broken skin.

5
Minutes 25–30

Steam & Softening (optional based on skin type)

Facial steam for 5 minutes softens comedone plugs, opens follicular openings, and prepares skin for extractions. Maintain steam 12–15 inches from face. Not appropriate for: rosacea, highly sensitive skin, active acne with significant inflammation, dilated capillaries. Alternative: warm compress towels applied 2–3 minutes.

6
Minutes 30–40

Extractions (when appropriate)

Only perform extractions on: closed and open comedones (not inflamed pustules or cysts). Wrap fingers in tissue or use clean extractor tool. Apply gentle, even pressure around the comedone — never squeeze directly on top. Release if the comedone does not express within 2 attempts — forcing causes trauma, scarring, and spreading bacteria. Apply toner or antiseptic after extractions. Apply spot treatment (salicylic, BHA) to extracted areas.

7
Minutes 40–45

Facial Massage

Massage increases circulation, lymphatic drainage, product penetration, and client relaxation. Apply massage medium (oil, cream, or serum appropriate for skin type). Use these primary techniques in sequence: Effleurage — long, gliding strokes to warm tissue and spread product. Petrissage — gentle kneading for lymphatic drainage. Tapotement — light tapping/percussion to stimulate circulation. Friction — small circular movements to break up tension. Always move in upward and outward directions — never downward. Avoid aggressive pressure over recent injectables.

8
Minutes 45–53

Mask Application

Select mask based on skin type and treatment goals. Apply in smooth, even layer avoiding eye area. Leave according to product instructions (typically 10–15 minutes). Clay/kaolin: Oily, congested skin. Absorbs sebum, tightens temporarily. Hydrogel/sheet: All skin types. Maximum hydration, soothing, calming. Cream/sleeping mask: Dry, dehydrated, mature skin. Barrier support. Brightening (vitamin C, kojic): Hyperpigmentation, dullness. Calming (centella, oat): Sensitive, rosacea, post-extraction.

9
Minutes 53–58

Serum, Moisturizer & SPF Application

Remove mask with warm, damp sponges. Tone. Apply targeted serum (vitamin C, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, retinol — based on skin concern). Allow serum to absorb 60 seconds. Apply appropriate moisturizer for skin type. Apply SPF 50+ as final step (daytime appointments — always. Evening appointments: skip SPF, apply richer night cream instead). Apply eye cream with ring finger using gentle tapping motion.

10
Minutes 58–60

Post-Treatment Consultation

Sit client up slowly. Show results in magnifying mirror. Explain what you observed and what you did and why. Recommend 2 retail products maximum (not more — overwhelming). Schedule next appointment before they leave. Hand them printed post-care instructions. Ask for a Google review with your direct link — this is the moment. They are happy. Strike while it's warm.

Managing Common Skin Conditions in the Treatment Room

Acne-Prone Skin

Modify not avoid

Do: Salicylic cleanse + BHA toner. Enzyme exfoliation. Extractions of comedones only. High-frequency (germicidal). Clay mask. LED blue light. Niacinamide + zinc ampoule.

Do NOT: Extract inflamed pustules or cysts. Apply comedogenic oils. Use occlusive heavy masks.

Key rule: Never extract over an inflamed lesion — you spread bacteria and cause scarring.

Rosacea

Calm is the goal

Do: Cool or lukewarm water only. Gentle cream cleanser. No steam — ever. Centella, oat, or aloe mask. LED red light. Calming serum (azelaic, green tea). SPF 50+ always.

Do NOT: Steam, hot towels, heat, extractions, AHAs, high-frequency, friction massage, essential oils.

Key rule: Rosacea is a chronic condition — management, not cure. Trigger avoidance + barrier support.

Hyperpigmentation

Consistent treatment

Do: Brightening vitamin C ampoule. Mandelic or lactic acid exfoliation. Brightening mask. Niacinamide. Kojic acid. SPF 50+ — non-negotiable. IPL (Fitzpatrick I–III only).

Do NOT: Aggressive AHAs in Fitzpatrick IV–VI (PIH risk). Heat treatments over pigmented areas without test patch.

Key rule: SPF is the most important treatment for hyperpigmentation. Without it, every other treatment fails.

Advanced Esthetic Modalities
Microneedling, dermaplaning, high-frequency, LED, microcurrent, and gua sha — protocols and contraindications
Microneedling — Esthetic Scope (State-Dependent)

Scope of Practice Note

Microneedling laws vary significantly by state. Some states allow licensed estheticians to perform microneedling at specific depths (typically 0.25–0.5mm). Others require a medical license for any microneedling. Confirm your state's specific requirements before offering this service. Operating outside your licensed scope is a legal liability, not just a professional one.

Esthetic Microneedling ProtocolDetail
Depth range (esthetic scope)0.25–0.5mm — superficial epidermis to papillary dermis. Improves texture, tone, product absorption. Does not achieve deep collagen remodeling.
Topical numbingApply 30–45 minutes prior. Remove completely before beginning. Do NOT needle over numbing cream.
Passes2–4 passes in horizontal, vertical, and diagonal directions. Consistent pressure. Slight erythema (redness) = expected endpoint at esthetic depth.
Serums during treatmentApply hyaluronic acid serum throughout as lubricant and active delivery. GHK-Cu, growth factors, or vitamin C serums can be applied — they penetrate via channels.
Post-treatment careCool compress immediately. Hyaluronic acid serum. Calming moisturizer. SPF before leaving. No active ingredients (retinoids, AHAs, BHAs, vitamin C) for 5–7 days.
ContraindicationsActive acne breakout, active skin infection, keloid history, Accutane within 6–12 months, blood thinners, pregnancy, pacemaker (RF devices), rosacea flare, open wounds.
IntervalEvery 3–4 weeks for esthetic depth. Series of 4–6 for texture improvement.
High-Frequency

Direct Method (orange/red electrode)

  • Electrode applied directly to skin over gauze
  • Ozone produced = germicidal and anti-inflammatory
  • Best for: oily, acne-prone skin, post-extraction
  • Move in small circles — never hold stationary
  • Duration: 3–5 minutes total facial treatment

Indirect Method (Viennese massage)

  • Client holds electrode. Provider performs massage — current travels through both
  • Increases circulation, heat production, product penetration
  • Best for: mature, dry, devitalized skin
  • Apply oil or cream first — do not use on dry skin
  • Duration: 5–10 minutes

Post-Treatment Client Scripts — What to Say at Checkout

Post-Classic Facial Script

Say this every time

"Your skin may look a little flushed or feel slightly sensitive for the next few hours — that's completely normal. Tonight, keep it simple: gentle cleanser, moisturizer, and that's it. No retinoids, no vitamin C, no actives tonight. Starting tomorrow, go back to your normal routine. The most important thing: SPF 50 every single morning. Your skin is fresh and more sensitive to UV right now. Any extractions we did may have minor redness for 24–48 hours — that's healing. If you have any questions, text us. And I'm going to text you the Google review link — we'd be so grateful if you had a moment to share your experience. 🤍"

Post-Peel Script

The peel downtime conversation

"Your skin is going to peel over the next 3–7 days — and I need you to let it happen naturally. I know it's tempting to pick or peel — but please don't. Peeling on its own timeline means you're healing perfectly. Do not use retinoids, AHAs, BHAs, or any active ingredients until peeling has completely stopped. Gentle cleanser, thick moisturizer, and SPF 50+ every single day — even if you're inside. No saunas, steam, exercise, or any heat for 5 days. And no sun exposure — your skin is very vulnerable to UV right now. The best result comes from patient aftercare. If you follow these instructions, your results will be significantly better."

Post-Microneedling Script

Setting expectations correctly

"Your skin is going to look red — similar to a sunburn — for 24–48 hours. Some people also have mild swelling, especially around the eyes. This is completely expected and means the treatment worked. For the next 72 hours: only the products I recommend (the hyaluronic acid and calming moisturizer). No makeup for 24 hours. No retinoids, acids, or active ingredients for 5–7 days. No exercise, saunas, or heat for 48 hours. SPF 50+ every day starting tomorrow. Your skin may peel or flake lightly around days 3–5 — normal. Don't pick. Results continue developing for 4–6 weeks as collagen rebuilds. Your next session should be in 4–6 weeks for best results."

The Standards Every Esthetic Provider Must Hold

Non-Negotiables in the Treatment Room

  • Fresh linens for every client — no exceptions
  • Never perform treatment without completed consent form
  • Never give advice outside your scope of practice
  • Document every treatment in the chart the same day
  • Never extract inflamed acne lesions — only comedones
  • Never use expired products or medications
  • Sharps disposal in approved containers immediately
  • Ask for a Google review from every satisfied client

The Client Experience Standards

  • Greet client by name — always use their name
  • Explain every step before you do it — no surprises
  • Check in at least twice during treatment — "How does that feel?"
  • Recommend maximum 2 retail products — not more
  • Book the next appointment before they leave the room
  • 24-hour follow-up text — "How is your skin feeling?"
  • Never say "I don't know" without following up immediately
  • Every complaint goes to the provider/owner same day
