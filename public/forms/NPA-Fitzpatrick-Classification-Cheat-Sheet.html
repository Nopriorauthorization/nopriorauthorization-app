Filler & Botox Considerations by Skin Type
All Fitzpatrick typesInjectables themselves have no Fitzpatrick-specific contraindication — technique, not skin color, determines safety
Bruising visibilityBruising more visible in lighter skin (I–II). Darker skin (V–VI) may show bruising differently — purple to black tones
Keloid riskPatients with Fitzpatrick V–VI have higher keloid/hypertrophic scar risk. Relevant for any puncture — use smallest gauge appropriate
PIH from injectionAny injection that causes significant trauma or inflammation can trigger PIH in darker skin types — use fine gauge, minimize trauma
Tyndall effectBlue-gray discoloration from superficial HA is MORE visible in Fitzpatrick I–III due to lighter overlying skin — inject at correct depth
Assessment Tips — How to Determine Fitzpatrick Type
★ Ask: "What happens to your skin when you go in the sun without protection for 30 minutes?" — the answer is the most reliable classifier
★ Do NOT classify by skin color alone — a medium-toned patient who never burns is Type IV or V behaviorally, even if they appear lighter
★ Mixed heritage patients often have different Fitzpatrick responses than expected from appearance — always ask
★ Document the Fitzpatrick type in every patient chart — it affects laser settings, peel selection, and treatment planning permanently
