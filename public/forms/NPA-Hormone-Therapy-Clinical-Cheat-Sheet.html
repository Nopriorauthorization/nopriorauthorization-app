TIMING
Draw labs at trough for pellets (just before next insertion). Topical/gel — draw 2–4 hours after application to capture peak. IM — draw at trough (day before next injection).
🚫 Contraindications
ABS-E
Estrogen absolute CI: Breast cancer (current or history). Endometrial cancer. Unexplained vaginal bleeding. Active VTE or PE. Active liver disease. Stroke or MI within 12 months.
ABS-T
Testosterone absolute CI (men): Prostate cancer. Breast cancer. Severe BPH with urinary symptoms. Hematocrit >54%.
REL
Relative CI: BRCA1/2 carriers — shared decision-making. Uncontrolled HTN. Severe obesity (BMI >40) — higher VTE risk. Migraines with aura — transdermal preferred over oral.
TIMING
WHI study context: WHI used conjugated equine estrogen + MPA (synthetic) — NOT bioidentical HRT. Do not apply WHI risk data directly to BHRT. Evidence base differs.
📋 Symptom Screening Checklist
FEMALE
Menopausal symptoms: Hot flashes, night sweats, vaginal dryness, dyspareunia, sleep disruption, mood changes, brain fog, fatigue, decreased libido, weight gain, hair thinning, dry skin.
MALE
Hypogonadism symptoms: Low energy/fatigue, decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, mood changes/depression, decreased muscle mass, increased body fat, poor concentration, hot flashes, night sweats.
TOOLS
Use validated symptom tools: MENQOL (menopause quality of life), Menopause Rating Scale (MRS), ADAM questionnaire (men). Document baseline for treatment tracking.
💉 Pellet Therapy Protocol
SITE
Upper outer buttock or flank. Local anesthetic (lidocaine 1%). Small trocar incision. Pellet inserted subcutaneously. Steri-strip + pressure dressing.
POST
48 hours: No swimming, hot tubs, soaking. No strenuous exercise involving insertion site. Keep dry. No pressure on site.
INTERVAL
Women: q3–4 months. Men: q4–6 months. Draw labs 4 weeks before next insertion (trough level) to guide dosing adjustment.
EXTRUSION
Pellet extrusion risk: ~2–3%. Signs: redness, discharge, palpable pellet near surface. Maintain pressure, wound care. May need removal if symptomatic.
FIBROSIS
Scar tissue from repeated insertions at same site — rotate sites. Use same buttock but different location each insertion. Document site used.

🧠 High-Yield Clinical Pearls
★
Transdermal > oral estrogen for VTE risk. Always prefer transdermal unless patient-specific reason for oral.
★
Progesterone protects the uterus. Never give systemic estrogen to intact uterus without progestogen. Non-negotiable.
★
WHI data ≠ BHRT data. Understand the difference. Educate patients who present with fear from outdated media coverage.
★
Hematocrit monitoring in men on testosterone is critical. Polycythemia = increased stroke/clot risk. Hold if Hct >54%.
★
Dose to symptoms, not just labs. A patient with E2 of 60 who feels terrible may need 100. The number is a guide, not the goal.
★
Mammogram required before initiating estrogen therapy in women. Annual mammography during therapy. Document in chart.
★
Collaborative practice agreement (if required by state) must be current before prescribing. Verify scope of practice laws annually.
★
Thyroid overlap: Hypothyroid and menopause symptoms are nearly identical. Always check TSH before attributing to hormone deficiency.
