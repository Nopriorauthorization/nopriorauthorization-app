Resource 01
The 90-Day Week-by-Week Launch Roadmap
Exactly what to do, in order, every week from license to launch — no guessing required
From Danielle
"The most overwhelming thing about opening a practice is not knowing what to do first. Everything feels equally urgent and you end up paralyzed. This roadmap fixes that. One week at a time. Check the box. Move forward. I would have given anything for this list in Year One."
Phase 1 — Foundation  ·  Weeks 1–4  ·  Before Anything Else

Week 1 — Legal & Business Structure

Do these before spending a dollar anywhere else
Form your LLC or PLLC — file with your state Secretary of State online. Takes 1–3 days. Cost: $50–$500 depending on state.
Apply for your EIN — free at IRS.gov. Takes 10 minutes online. You need this before you can open a business bank account.
Open a business bank account — separate from personal. Bring your EIN, LLC paperwork, and ID. Never comingle personal and business funds.
Consult a healthcare attorney — 1 hour paid consultation. Confirm scope of practice for your license type in your state. Worth every dollar.
Research collaborative practice agreement (CPA) requirements — does your state require physician oversight for medical services? Get the answer in writing from your state medical board.

Week 2 — Insurance & Licensing

You cannot see a single patient without these
Apply for professional liability insurance — use a med spa specialty broker. Confirm every service you plan to offer is covered explicitly. Do not assume.
Apply for general liability insurance — required by most commercial leases. Separate from professional liability.
Confirm your facility license requirements — some states require a clinic license for any practice performing injections. Check with your state Department of Health.
Verify your professional license is active and in good standing — confirm on your state board website. Screenshot and save.
Apply for city/county business license and any required permits — check your local municipality's requirements.

Week 3 — Space, Equipment & Supplies

Minimum viable setup to see your first patient
Secure your treatment space — lease, sublease, or suite rental. Confirm your lease allows the services you plan to offer. Get a lawyer to review before signing.
Order minimum viable equipment — treatment table/chair, lighting, magnification lamp, medical-grade refrigerator (if storing injectables), sharps containers, supply cart.
Set up a medical waste disposal vendor — Stericycle or local equivalent. Required before your first needle. Cannot use regular trash for sharps.
Order consumable supplies — gloves, syringes, alcohol wipes, gauze, needles, topical numbing, PPE. See the NPA Vendor Directory for suppliers.
Set up your booking system — Fresha (free), Vagaro, or Jane App. Configure services, pricing, cancellation policy, and online booking before you announce anything.

Week 4 — HIPAA, OSHA & Documentation

The paperwork that protects you and your patients
Draft your Notice of Privacy Practices (HIPAA NPP) — required before your first patient. Every patient receives a copy at their first visit.
Write your Bloodborne Pathogen Exposure Control Plan — required by OSHA before you hire anyone or use a needle. Free templates at OSHA.gov.
Set up your consent form system — one consent form per service type. Use the NPA Consent Form Bundle as your starting point.
Set up your patient intake form system — medical history, medication list, allergy history, photo consent, communication preferences.
Create your Google Business Profile — claim or create at business.google.com. Add photos, hours, services, and your booking link. This is how people find you.
Phase 2 — Pre-Launch  ·  Weeks 5–8  ·  Build Before You Open

Weeks 5–6 — Your First Patients & Social Presence

Get patients before you officially open
Create your Instagram and Facebook business accounts — use your practice name. Link them together. Start posting coming soon content (see Resource 05).
Join 3–5 local Facebook groups — neighborhood groups, local moms groups, community groups. Introduce yourself as a new local business. Do not sell. Just connect.
Offer 10 "founding patient" spots — limited slots, discounted rate or added value, first access when you open. Announce in your groups and to your personal network.
Set up your patient financing accounts — apply to become a CareCredit and Cherry accepting provider. Financing increases your average transaction by 30–50%.
Apply for product accounts — Allergan (Botox/Juvederm), Galderma (Dysport/Restylane), EltaMD, your skincare lines. These take time — apply early.

Weeks 7–8 — Final Prep & Soft Launch

Everything in place — test your systems before the world sees you
Do 5–10 practice treatments — friends, family, or models who understand they're your first paid or discounted patients. Get comfortable with your workflow, room, and documentation.
Take before/after photos of every practice patient — with consent. These become your first social media content. See the NPA Before/After Photo System.
Ask every practice patient for a Google review — use the script in Resource 04. Your first 5–10 reviews before you officially launch changes everything.
Test your booking system end to end — book yourself as a test patient. Confirm the confirmation email, reminder texts, and intake form link all work correctly.
Set your official opening date — announce on social media 1 week before. Build anticipation. Use the content calendar in Resource 05.
Phase 3 — Launch & Early Growth  ·  Weeks 9–12

Weeks 9–12 — Open, Operate & Build Momentum

You're open. Now build the habits that make you great.
Ask every single patient for a Google review — in person + text link within 5 minutes of departure. Every week. Non-negotiable. This is how you build a 4.9.
Post to social media 5 days per week — use the content calendar. Consistency beats quality. Show up every day even when you don't feel like it.
Schedule every follow-up appointment before the patient leaves — Botox 14-day follow-up. Filler 14-day follow-up. Next treatment 3–4 months. Retention starts at checkout.
Track your weekly numbers — appointments booked, appointments completed, revenue, new patients, reviews received. Know your numbers from Week 1.
Introduce your membership program — present to every happy patient at checkout. "Have you heard about our membership? After seeing your results today, it might be perfect for you."
Resource 02
The Business Setup Checklist
The exact sequence — nothing missed, nothing out of order
Entity & Legal Foundation

Form LLC or PLLC (before anything else)

File with your state Secretary of State. Most states have online filing. Bring your name, registered agent address, and member information. Cost: $50–$500.

Apply for EIN (Employee Identification Number)

Free at IRS.gov — takes 10 minutes. Apply immediately after LLC is confirmed. You need this for everything that follows.

Open dedicated business checking account

Bring your EIN, LLC articles, and personal ID. Never use personal account for business. Commingling funds = losing your LLC protection.

Draft Operating Agreement (LLC) or Bylaws (Corp)

Defines how your business is governed, ownership, and decision-making. Essential if you have any business partners. Have an attorney draft or review.

Hire a CPA who specializes in healthcare small business

Set up your bookkeeping, understand your tax structure, and make sure you're not leaving money on the table from Day One.

Licensing & Compliance

Confirm professional license is active in your state

Screenshot confirmation from your state board website. Keep this on file.

Confirm scope of practice for every service you plan to offer

Call your state medical board AND esthetics board. Get the answer in writing. Do not rely on Facebook group advice.

Determine if CPA / medical director is required

Check your state NP practice authority level. If required — get it drafted by a healthcare attorney before offering any prescription service.

Obtain city/county business license

Check your local municipality's requirements. Usually simple online application. Required before operating.

Check if facility license is required in your state

Contact your state Department of Health. Some states require a clinic license for any practice performing injections or IV therapy.

Apply for DEA registration (if prescribing controlled substances)

Required for testosterone, ketamine, and other scheduled substances. Apply at DEA.gov. Per-location registration.

Insurance Stack (all required before first patient)

Required Insurance

  • Professional liability (malpractice) — covers treatment-related claims
  • General liability — covers slip/fall, premises incidents
  • Workers' comp — required the moment you hire anyone
  • Cyber liability — covers HIPAA breach response costs

Booking & Software Setup

  • Booking system configured (Fresha, Vagaro, or Jane App)
  • Online booking live with correct services and prices
  • Cancellation policy enabled with card on file requirement
  • Intake form link configured and tested
  • Payment processing set up and tested
Digital Presence — Do This Before You Open

Claim/create your Google Business Profile

business.google.com → claim or create → verify your address → add photos, hours, services, booking link. This is the single most important digital asset you own.

Create Instagram and Facebook business accounts

Use your exact practice name. Link Instagram to your Facebook Page. Add booking link to both bios.

Purchase your domain name

YourPracticeName.com — buy it even if you're not ready to build a website yet. Protects your brand. Cost: $12–15/year.

Set up a professional email address

hello@yourpracticename.com — not a personal Gmail. Google Workspace costs $6/month. Looks professional immediately.

Resource 03
Your First 5 Services Pricing Workbook
Walk through your own numbers — price your services with confidence from Day One
From Danielle
"I underpriced everything for my first two years because I didn't know my numbers. I felt like I had to be cheap because I was new. What I know now: patients associate low prices with low quality. Know your cost, know your value, price accordingly — and never apologize for it."
The Pricing Formula — 5 Steps

Step 1 — Calculate your Cost of Goods (COGS)

What does it actually cost you to perform this service? Include: product (units of Botox × your cost per unit, filler cost), consumables (syringes, alcohol wipes, gauze, gloves), and any product applied topically during the treatment.

Step 2 — Calculate your Labor Cost

Your hourly rate × time for this service. If you're the provider: what do you want to pay yourself per hour? Example: $100/hr provider rate × 30-minute service = $50 labor cost for that appointment.

Step 3 — Allocate Overhead

Monthly overhead (rent, utilities, software, insurance, supplies) ÷ monthly appointment capacity = overhead per appointment slot. Multiply by % of overhead this service uses.

Step 4 — Set Your Target Margin

Target 40–60% net margin on services after COGS, labor, and overhead. Add-on services (retail, memberships) have higher margins. Anchor services (Botox, filler) should hit at least 40%.

Step 5 — Market Check

What are 3 comparable providers in your market charging? Price within 10–20% of market for comparable quality. If you're positioning as premium — price at or above market and deliver the experience that justifies it.

The Most Important Pricing Rule

If every client says yes without hesitation — you are underpriced. If no one ever pushes back — raise your prices. A healthy practice has occasional price sensitivity from patients. That's normal. Resource 04
The First 30 Patients Playbook
How to get patients before you have reviews — Danielle's exact Year One strategy
From Danielle
"Getting my first 30 patients was the hardest thing I've ever done in business. I had no reviews, no reputation, no one knew who I was. Here's exactly what I did — and what I would do today if I was starting over from zero."
Week 1–2: Your Warm Network (Patients 1–10)

Text 20 people in your personal network today

Not a group text — individual messages. "I'm opening [Practice Name] and I'd love for you to be one of my first patients. I'm offering a founding rate of [X] for the first 10 people who book. Would you want to come in?" Personal beats everything.

Post your "I'm opening" announcement on your personal social media

Not your business page — your personal profile. Your friends and family are your first audience. Tell your story. Why you did this. What you offer. Booking link. This one post will get you more patients than your first 30 business page posts combined.

Offer a "Founding Patient Special"

First 10–15 patients get a specific benefit: 15% off their first service, a complimentary add-on, or a gift with purchase. Create real urgency with a real cap. "I only have 15 founding spots and 8 are already claimed."

Ask every person who books to bring a friend

"If you bring a friend to your appointment, you both get [X]." Word of mouth from your first 10 patients is worth more than any ad you'll ever run.

Week 3–6: Facebook Groups (Patients 10–25)
Local Facebook groups are the highest-converting free marketing channel for a new med spa. Not because you're advertising — but because you're becoming a trusted presence in your community. Here's the exact strategy.

Join 5–8 local Facebook groups

Search: "[Your City] Moms," "[Your City] Community," "[Your City] Girls/Women," "[Your City] Deals and Recommendations," "[Your County] Neighbors." Join as yourself — not your business page.

The 20-minute-per-day engagement strategy

Every day: (1) Answer one question someone asked that you have expertise in. (2) Comment genuinely on 3 posts. (3) Post one piece of value — a skincare tip, a myth bust, a question for the community. Never sell directly. Build recognition first.

When someone asks for a recommendation — that's your moment

When someone asks "does anyone know a good injector near [city]?" or "who does Botox around here?" — respond immediately. "Hi! I'm [Name], I actually just opened [Practice Name] right in [city]. I'd love to offer you a complimentary consultation. DM me anytime." Professional, warm, not pushy.

Introduce yourself to the group — one time, done right

"Hi everyone! I'm [Name], a [credential] who just opened [Practice Name] in [city]. I specialize in [services]. I know how personal these decisions are and I'm committed to building real relationships in this community — not just booking appointments. Happy to answer any questions about aesthetics/skincare/wellness anytime!" Include a warm photo of yourself.

Week 6–12: Reviews, Referrals & Momentum (Patients 25–30+)

Ask every single patient for a Google review — in person + text

While they're in the chair looking in the mirror happy: "Can I ask you a huge favor? If you have 60 seconds before you leave, a Google review means everything to a new small practice like mine — it's literally how people find us." Text the link within 5 minutes.

Create a referral incentive

"For every friend you send who books, you get [X]." Track referrals. Thank the referring patient personally. Referral patients are your highest-lifetime-value patients because they arrive with trust already built.

Cross-promote with complementary local businesses

Introduce yourself to: hair salons, nail salons, gyms, boutiques, chiropractors, and yoga studios near you. Offer to display their cards if they display yours. Refer clients to each other. Community beats competition every time.

Post your first before/afters (with consent)

Your first real results posted on social media — even modest ones — generate more interest than any promotional post. People want to see what you actually do. Post them with a personal caption about why you love your work.

The mindset shift that changes everything: Your first 30 patients are not customers. They are your founding community. Treat them like family. Overdeliver on every experience. Make them feel like the most important people who walked through your door — because they are. They will tell 10 people. Those 10 will tell 10 more. That's how a practice is built.

Resource 06
Danielle's Opening Day Letter
The one page she would hand her Year One self — for you, on yours

To you — on the day you open your doors,

You're not ready. I need you to know that — not to scare you, but to free you. Nobody is ready on Day One. I wasn't. The providers you admire weren't. Readiness is a myth we tell ourselves to delay starting things that scare us. You have already done the thing that matters most: you showed up.

Your first patient is going to walk through that door and they are going to be nervous. And so will you. And that's okay — because that nervousness is the same thing as caring. Providers who stop being nervous are providers who've stopped caring. Never let that happen.

You will make mistakes this year. Not big ones — but you'll second-guess an injection, misjudge a result, or handle a difficult conversation the wrong way. When that happens: own it, fix it, learn from it, and move forward. The providers who grow the fastest are not the ones who never make mistakes. They're the ones who turn every mistake into a lesson.

The money will feel terrifying for a while. There will be slow weeks and you will wonder if you made the right decision. You did. But the business takes longer to grow than you think it will — and it always grows faster than you think once it starts. Trust the process. Show up every day. Ask every patient for a review. Take every before/after photo. Send every follow-up text. The consistency of small actions is what builds a great practice — not one viral post.

Be the provider that knows their patients' names. Be the one who remembers what they were worried about last time and asks how it went. Be the one who calls personally when something goes wrong. Relationships are your product. The Botox and the filler are just the vehicle.

Protect your prices. Protect your time. Protect your energy. You cannot pour from an empty cup and you cannot serve your patients well if you're burnt out, undercharging, and overworked. Charge what you're worth. Say no to patients who don't respect your practice. Build boundaries early — they are much harder to build later.

And on the days when it's hard — and there will be hard days — remember why you started. Not the license. Not the income. The thing that made you say yes to this. For me it was my aunt. For you it's something real and true and yours. Go back to that place when you need to. It will always tell you what to do next.

You are more ready than you know. You are more capable than you feel today. And ten years from now, you will look back at this moment — the fear, the excitement, the not-knowing — and you will be so grateful you walked through that door anyway.

Now go take care of someone. 🤍

— Danielle Alcala
Founder, Hello Gorgeous Med Spa · Founder, No Prior Authorization · Oswego, IL
