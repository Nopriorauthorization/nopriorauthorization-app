Read this first — why this matters for Danielle specifically

You are a licensed esthetician and medical student. You are NOT a licensed physician, nurse practitioner, or prescribing provider in your own right at this time. Ryan Kent, FNP-BC, is a collaborating clinical partner. NPA publishes clinical content — hormone therapy, peptide therapy, GLP-1 protocols, consent forms — that is educational in nature. Without proper disclaimers, a buyer could attempt to claim they relied on NPA content as a substitute for professional medical advice and suffered harm. The disclaimers below create a clear legal boundary: NPA is educational. It is not medical advice. It does not create a provider-patient relationship. Every clinical product, every playbook, and every page on the site must carry appropriate language.

What disclaimers protect you from

What NPA is — protected

  • Educational content for licensed providers
  • Operational business systems and templates
  • Clinical frameworks for providers to adapt with their own judgment
  • Digital products — not medical services
  • General information — not individualized medical advice

What NPA is NOT — disclaim clearly

  • A licensed medical practice
  • A substitute for provider-patient consultation
  • Legal, regulatory, or compliance advice
  • A guarantee of clinical outcomes
  • A replacement for state-specific scope verification
  • FDA-approved clinical guidance
The three layers of protection you need

Layer 1 — Site-wide disclaimer

Covers the entire website. Lives in the footer on every page. Sets the global expectation that NPA is educational, not medical advice.

Layer 2 — Clinical product disclaimers

Specific to hormone therapy, peptide therapy, GLP-1, and other clinical playbooks. States clearly that content is for licensed prescribing providers only and does not replace clinical judgment.

Layer 3 — Consent form disclaimers

On every consent form template — states these are template frameworks requiring attorney review and provider customization before clinical use. Not legal documents as-is.

Attorney review — what to do after building this

These disclaimers are written to cover the primary legal risks for a digital health education platform. Before launch, have a healthcare or digital business attorney in Illinois review the site-wide disclaimer and the clinical product disclaimers. This is a one-time cost of $300–$800 and is worth every dollar. Ask specifically about: scope of practice language, FTC compliance for digital health products, and the consent form template disclaimer language.

First-visit acknowledgment banner — how it works

How Anthony builds this

A sticky banner at the bottom of the screen on first visit — similar to a cookie consent bar. Black background, white text, pink CTA. When the user clicks "I understand — continue to site" the banner dismisses and a flag is set in localStorage so it never appears again for that browser. Simple. Non-intrusive. Legally important.

The privacy policy and terms — what to use

Recommended approach for /privacy and /terms

  • Termly (termly.io) — $10/month, generates GDPR and CCPA compliant privacy policy and terms of use. Paste the embed code. Most digital businesses use this. Covers data collection, cookies, email list, purchases.
  • Iubenda (iubenda.com) — similar, also generates cookie policy. Good alternative.
  • Must include: what data NPA collects (email, purchase history), how it's used (Mailchimp, Supabase, Stripe), cookies, third-party processors, user rights (delete account, unsubscribe), contact information.
  • Attorney note: Privacy policy and terms of use should also be reviewed by an attorney before launch — especially the limitation of liability clause in the terms.
