Before Day One

What you do before they arrive sets the tone for everything that follows

Hiring right — what to look for beyond credentials

The non-negotiables beyond licensure

  • Emotional intelligence — do they listen before they speak?
  • Humility — are they willing to say "I don't know"?
  • Attention to detail — small mistakes in this field have real consequences
  • Patient rapport — watch how they interact with non-clinical staff during the interview
  • Coachability — do they receive feedback gracefully or defensively?
Ryan's interview question
"Tell me about a time you made a mistake with a patient. What happened, and what did you do?"
The answer tells you everything. A candidate who can't recall a mistake hasn't been honest with themselves. A candidate who blames the patient, the system, or someone else won't be coachable. You want someone who owns it, learned from it, and changed.

Week One — Observe Everything, Touch Nothing

The first week is about absorbing the culture, the systems, and the patients

What to say on day one
"Welcome. This week, your only job is to watch and ask questions. You won't be touching a patient. You won't be charting independently. You're going to shadow every consultation, every injection, every follow-up call. The goal this week isn't to learn procedures — it's to understand how we think about patients here."
New injectors who are eager to start injecting on day one are the ones who miss the culture piece. The clinical skills can be developed — the patient relationship philosophy has to be absorbed.
Monday

Orientation day

  • Facility tour — treatment rooms, emergency equipment locations, supply storage
  • Review emergency protocols — anaphylaxis, vasovagal, vascular occlusion
  • EMR walkthrough — how to access patient records, document a visit, pull consent forms
  • Meet the full team — understand every role and how they interact
  • Complete HIPAA training and sign acknowledgment
Tue–Wed

Shadow consultations

  • Observe minimum 8–10 consultations across different treatment types
  • Note: how Ryan opens, how he handles hesitant patients, how he sets expectations
  • Review consent forms — understand every section and why it exists
  • After each consult: debrief with mentor — what did you notice? what would you have done differently?
Thu–Fri

Shadow treatments

  • Observe minimum 10–15 injections — Botox and filler
  • Study injection mapping — where Ryan marks, why he marks there
  • Review post-treatment documentation — how a chart is completed correctly
  • End of week: written reflection — 3 things learned, 1 question still open, 1 thing that surprised you

End of week 1 — check-in conversation

"How are you feeling? What's clicked? What feels overwhelming? What do you wish you knew before you started?" This conversation prevents small concerns from becoming week-4 problems. Do it in person, not via text, and take notes.

2

Week Two — Participate, Don't Lead

Hands on anatomy models, assisting on treatments, beginning to document

Mon–Tue

Anatomy and injection technique drills

  • Facial anatomy review — name every muscle, its action, its antagonist
  • Practice injection technique on training models or oranges — angle, depth, pressure control
  • Reconstitution drill — practice reconstituting vials to spec, labeling correctly
  • Quiz: dosing ranges for all standard Botox areas — must score 90%+ before proceeding
Wed–Thu

Assisted patient care

  • Assist with patient prep — vitals, intake review, photo documentation
  • Observe injections at close range — note needle angle, depth, pace
  • Complete patient charts under mentor review — mentor checks and corrects before finalizing
  • Practice injection mapping on mentor before patient — mark placement, discuss rationale
Friday

First supervised injection — glabella only

  • Mentor identifies appropriate patient — established, calm, good candidate for new injector
  • Full patient disclosure: "We have a new provider training with us — Ryan will be present the entire time"
  • New injector performs glabella injection under direct supervision
  • Immediate debrief — what went well, what to adjust
  • Mentor completes or co-signs the chart

If they're not ready — say it clearly and kindly

"I want to give you a little more time before we move to patient injections — I've seen a few things this week that I want us to work through first. That's not a setback, that's me protecting you and our patients. Let's use Friday for more model practice and try patient injections next week." Rushing an underprepared injector is how you create a complication.

3

Weeks Three & Four — Graduated Independence

Expanding treatment zones, increasing autonomy, building confidence safely

Week 3 — expanding the treatment map
1

Add forehead and crow's feet — supervised

Glabella mastered in week 2. Week 3 adds upper face treatment. Mentor present for all sessions. Debrief after every patient.

2

First filler observation and assist

Filler is not week-3 independent injection territory. Observe and assist only. Understand the anatomy, the product selection, the technique — hands come later.

3

Consultation practice — lead with mentor present

New injector leads the consultation. Mentor observes and does not interrupt unless safety is a concern. Debrief privately after. Grade on: listening, assessment quality, expectation setting, contraindication screening.

4

Handle a 2-week follow-up call — supervised

Listen to how they communicate outcomes, handle concerns, and rebook. This is a skill that takes practice. Role-play difficult scenarios before they take a live call.

Week 4 — first solo patients

Criteria for solo clearance

  • Demonstrated safe, accurate glabella and forehead injection technique across 10+ supervised patients
  • Zero unaddressed documentation errors in week 3
  • Passed verbal complication protocol exam — can describe response to ptosis, vasovagal, vascular occlusion
  • Led 3+ consultations with mentor approval
  • Mentor signs off in writing before first solo session
Clearing them for solo — what to say
"You're ready. I've watched you carefully over the past four weeks and I'm confident in you. Here's what I want you to remember: you can always call me. If something doesn't look right, if a patient is reacting unexpectedly, if you're unsure about anything — stop and call. There's no scenario where calling me is the wrong move. I'm always a phone call away."
This moment matters more than you think. How you hand off a new injector determines how they practice for the rest of their career. Make it intentional.

Clinical Standards & Non-Negotiables

The rules every injector at this practice follows — no exceptions

The NPA standard of care

These are not suggestions — they are requirements

  • Photos before every treatment — no exceptions, no patient refusals accepted without documented informed declination
  • Signed consent before every treatment — even returning patients, even "quick" touch-ups
  • Medical history reviewed at every visit — not just new patients
  • Lot numbers documented for every injectable used
  • Never inject when you are uncertain — stop, call for support, never guess
  • Hyaluronidase must be on-site before any HA filler is injected
  • Anaphylaxis kit must be stocked and checked monthly
Patient communication standards

How we talk to patients here

  • First name always — introduce yourself by first name, use the patient's first name
  • No rushing — a patient who feels hurried feels uncared for
  • Validate before you educate — acknowledge what they said before you respond clinically
  • Never dismiss a concern as "just anxiety" — investigate it
  • No phone at the treatment table — ever
  • If you make a mistake — own it to the patient immediately, report it internally the same day
Social media and patient privacy

What never happens here

No patient photos are shared on any platform — personal or professional — without a signed photo release on file. No discussing patient outcomes in any identifiable way in public or on social media. No filming in treatment rooms without explicit consent. Violations of patient privacy are grounds for immediate termination.

The culture statement — read this to every new hire
"We are a practice that puts patients before productivity, safety before speed, and honesty before approval. When something goes wrong — and eventually something will — we do not hide it, minimize it, or shift blame. We call the patient, we take responsibility, we fix it, and we learn from it. That's what separates a great practice from a mediocre one. Welcome to the team."

The 30-Day Performance Review

A structured conversation that sets up the next 60 days

Review framework — what to evaluate

Clinical performance

  • Documentation accuracy — are charts complete, timely, and compliant?
  • Technique consistency — are injection placements accurate and reproducible?
  • Complication awareness — can they identify and respond appropriately to adverse events?
  • Product knowledge — can they explain every product they're using to a patient clearly?

Patient experience

  • Consultation quality — are patients leaving informed and confident?
  • Patient satisfaction — any complaints or compliments in the first 30 days?
  • Rebooking rate — are their patients scheduling return visits?
  • Review generation — have they asked patients for Google reviews?

Team integration

  • Communication with staff — are they collaborative, respectful, clear?
  • Asking for help — do they know when to escalate vs. handle independently?
  • Culture fit — do they embody the practice values?
The conversation itself
How to open the 30-day review
"Before I share any of my observations, I want to hear from you first. How do you feel like the first 30 days went? What are you proud of? What do you wish had gone differently? What do you need from me to set you up for a great month two?"
Start with their perspective every time. It accomplishes three things: it shows respect, it surfaces issues you might not have seen, and it makes them more receptive to your feedback when you deliver it. This is not a performance improvement plan — it is a growth conversation.

The 60-day roadmap — set it together

End the 30-day review with a written agreement: 3 specific clinical goals for month two, 1 patient experience goal, and 1 personal development goal. Sign it together. Review it at 60 days. Injectors who have a clear roadmap perform better than those who are simply told to "keep doing what you're doing."
