Patient education

BPC-157 — what patients should know

BPC-157 is a peptide discussed in wellness and regenerative settings, often around tissue recovery and gut comfort. It is not FDA-approved as a standard drug for all the uses you may read about online. If your clinician recommends it, they should explain how it is sourced, whether it is compounded, and what evidence and regulations apply in your state.

Your provider’s role: Only a licensed prescriber working within their scope and your clinic’s compliance policies may order peptide therapies. Ask where the medication comes from, how it is stored, and what follow-up is planned.

What to expect from treatment

Plans differ by route (for example, injection under clinical supervision), frequency, and duration. Your team should set realistic expectations—peptides are not guaranteed fixes—and document your goals and progress.

Things to report before starting

  • All prescriptions, hormones, blood thinners, and supplements
  • Cancer history, pregnancy or breastfeeding, or plans to conceive
  • Prior reactions to injections or peptide products

Possible effects to watch for

Injection-site redness, bruising, or mild headache can occur. New swelling, dizziness, palpitations, or allergic symptoms should be reported promptly.

Follow-up

Keep scheduled visits so your provider can assess benefit, side effects, and whether to continue, adjust, or stop therapy.

This document is general patient education, not medical or legal advice. Regulatory status of compounded peptides can change; your clinic is responsible for current rules, informed consent, and prescribing. No Prior Authorization does not practice medicine.
