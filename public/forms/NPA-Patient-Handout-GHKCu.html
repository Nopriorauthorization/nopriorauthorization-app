Patient education

GHK-Cu — skin and wellness context

GHK-Cu (copper peptide) appears in aesthetic and skin-care conversations because of interest in collagen support, texture, and post-procedure recovery. Depending on your practice, it may be used topically, as part of in-office protocols, or in other forms your provider will explain. Results and timelines vary by person.

What you should clarify with your clinician

  • Whether your plan is topical only, in-office only, or combined—and why
  • How often to apply or return for treatment
  • What sun protection and home care are required between visits

Skin care at home

Use daily broad-spectrum SPF as directed. Avoid mixing active ingredients unless your provider approves. If you develop stinging, rash, or peeling that worsens, pause new products and call the office.

After in-office treatments

Follow written aftercare: cleansing, makeup avoidance, exercise limits, and follow-up timing. Mild redness or flaking can be expected with some protocols; spreading pain, fever, or blistering are not—seek guidance.

When to call

Allergic swelling, hives, or eye involvement; signs of infection; or lack of improvement when your provider said you should see change.
