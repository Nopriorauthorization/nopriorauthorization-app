Patient education

Glutathione (IV push / IM) — quick facts

Glutathione is an antioxidant your body makes naturally. Some wellness and aesthetic practices offer it as an injection or short IV push because patients ask about energy, recovery, and “detox” support. Your care team will explain whether this option fits your health history and goals.

What this treatment may involve

Depending on your plan, glutathione may be given as a slow IV push or an intramuscular (IM) shot. Visits are usually brief. Your provider will tell you the route, timing, and how many sessions make sense for you—there is no one-size-fits-all schedule.

What you might notice

  • Mild warmth, metallic taste, or lightheadedness during or right after the infusion—report anything that feels off.
  • Spot soreness or a small bruise at an IM injection site.
  • Serious allergic reactions are uncommon but possible; your team should observe you per office policy.

Safety topics to discuss with your provider

Mention asthma or reactive airway disease, all medications and supplements (including OTC), pregnancy or breastfeeding, and any history of allergies to injectable ingredients. Your clinic will document contraindications and alternatives that apply to you.

After your visit

Drink water, eat if you were fasting, and avoid strenuous activity until you feel normal. Follow any instructions your clinician gives for the rest of the day.

When to call the office

  • Trouble breathing, wheezing, throat tightness, or widespread hives
  • Chest pain, fainting, or severe or worsening headache
  • Fever, redness, or spreading pain at an injection site
