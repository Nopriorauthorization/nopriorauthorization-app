Patient education

NAD+ therapy — what to expect

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme involved in how cells produce energy. Patients often hear about NAD+ for fatigue, mental clarity, recovery, or healthy aging. Your provider will decide if IV or other forms of NAD+ are appropriate for you and at what pace to administer them.

During your infusion

NAD+ is usually given slowly through an IV because faster rates are more likely to cause uncomfortable sensations. Infusion length varies by protocol and dose. Expect to be seated or reclined; your team may check on you regularly.

Common sensations (tell your nurse right away)

  • Chest tightness, pressure, or pounding heartbeat
  • Nausea, flushing, warmth, or anxiety
  • Headache or lightheadedness

Most symptoms ease when the infusion is slowed or paused. Never hesitate to speak up—your comfort and safety come first.

Before you start

Share your full medication and supplement list, heart or blood pressure history, pregnancy or breastfeeding status, and any prior reactions to IV therapies. Your clinic may adjust the plan or recommend a different approach.

After your visit

Rest, hydrate, and eat as directed. Some people feel tired or wired for a short time afterward—follow your office’s guidance on work, exercise, and driving.

When to call the office or seek urgent care

  • Chest pain, fainting, or severe shortness of breath
  • Symptoms that do not improve after the infusion is stopped
  • Signs of infection at the IV site (spreading redness, pus, fever)
