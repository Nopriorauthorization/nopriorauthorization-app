GLP-1 (semaglutide) — patient overview

Semaglutide belongs to a class of medications called GLP-1 receptor agonists. In appropriate candidates, prescribers may use it to support weight management alongside nutrition, activity, and follow-up. It works on appetite and blood-sugar regulation pathways; it is not a “quick fix,” and results take time.

Why dose changes are gradual

Your clinician will usually increase your dose slowly to improve tolerance and monitor safety. Skipping steps or using someone else’s dose can raise the risk of side effects and is never appropriate.

Common side effects

  • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, or bloating
  • Decreased appetite (expected to some degree)
  • Fatigue or reflux in some people

Sipping fluids, smaller meals, and timing adjustments may help—ask your team before changing anything on your own.

Serious risks to know about

GLP-1 drugs have boxed warnings and precautions that your prescriber must review with you (for example, history of certain thyroid tumors, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, kidney issues, or diabetic eye disease). Pregnancy is not compatible with starting these medications for weight loss; discuss family planning.

When to call urgently

  • Severe or persistent abdominal pain with or without vomiting (possible pancreatitis)
  • Symptoms of allergic reaction (throat swelling, trouble breathing, widespread rash)
  • Signs of dehydration from prolonged vomiting or diarrhea
  • New vision changes or severe headache

Staying on track

Keep all lab and follow-up appointments. Tell every healthcare provider that you use a GLP-1 medication before surgery or new prescriptions.
