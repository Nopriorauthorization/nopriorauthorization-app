What is Tirzepatide

Tirzepatide is the first dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist — activating two separate hormone pathways simultaneously. Clinical trials have shown tirzepatide produces greater average weight loss than semaglutide — up to 22.5% of total body weight in the Surmount-1 trial. Available as FDA-approved Mounjaro and Zepbound, or through licensed compounding pharmacies, tirzepatide represents the most effective medical weight loss medication currently available.

How It Works

Dual Receptor Action

Activates both GIP and GLP-1 receptors simultaneously — two complementary pathways that together produce greater appetite suppression and metabolic improvement than either alone.

🧠

What You Need to Know

Important before your treatment:

Tirzepatide requires gradual dose titration — starting at 2.5mg and increasing every 4 weeks based on tolerance and response.
GI side effects (nausea, diarrhea, constipation) are most common in the first 4–8 weeks and typically improve significantly.
Protein intake is critical — minimum 0.8–1g per pound of ideal body weight daily to preserve muscle mass during weight loss.
Weight regain occurs when tirzepatide is discontinued. This medication manages a chronic biological condition.
Contraindications: personal or family history of MTC or MEN 2, history of pancreatitis, pregnancy. Disclose full medical history.

Frequently Asked Questions
How is tirzepatide different from semaglutide?
Tirzepatide activates two hormone receptors (GIP + GLP-1) vs semaglutide's one. Clinical trials show tirzepatide produces greater average weight loss — up to 22.5% vs ~15% for semaglutide.
Is tirzepatide right for me if semaglutide didn't work?
Many patients who had limited success with semaglutide respond very well to tirzepatide due to the dual mechanism. Ask your provider if switching makes sense.
How is it administered?
Tirzepatide is injected subcutaneously once weekly. Rotation between injection sites — abdomen, thigh, upper arm — is recommended.
What are the most common side effects?
Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation — most common in the first 4–8 weeks during titration. These improve significantly as your body adjusts.
Is compounded tirzepatide the same as Zepbound?
Compounded tirzepatide contains the same active ingredient but is not FDA-approved as a finished product. It is prepared by a licensed compounding pharmacy under your prescription.
What results should I realistically expect?
Clinical trials show 15–22.5% total body weight loss at maximum doses over 72 weeks. Results vary by individual, starting weight, and adherence.
