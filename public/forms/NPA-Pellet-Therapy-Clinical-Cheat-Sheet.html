💉 Insertion Protocol — Step by Step
SITE
Upper outer buttock / posterior flank. 1–2 inches below iliac crest, lateral to midline. Mark site with pen. Rotate sides each insertion — document which side used.
PREP
Betadine or chlorhexidine prep — wide area. Sterile drape. Lidocaine 1–2% with epi: 1–2 mL subQ + IM along trocar track. Wait 2–3 min for full anesthesia.
INCISION
Small stab incision with #11 blade — 3–4mm for women, 4–5mm for men. Bevel up, parallel to skin surface. Depth through subQ only — not into fascia.
TROCAR
Insert trocar at 30–45° angle through incision, advancing horizontally in subQ plane. Women: advance 2–3cm. Men: advance 3–4cm (larger pellets need more depth). Use obturator to deploy pellet.
CONFIRM
Withdraw trocar while keeping obturator in place. Palpate site — pellet should not be palpable if properly placed. If palpable and superficial, reposition or note for monitoring.
CLOSE
Steri-strip closure — 2–3 strips perpendicular to incision. Pressure dressing (folded gauze + tegaderm or athletic tape). Hold firm pressure 2–3 minutes before dressing.
MULTIPLE
Multiple pellets: Deploy through same incision — partial withdrawal, redirect slightly, deploy each pellet sequentially. Fan technique. Avoids multiple incisions.
📋 Post-Insertion Instructions
48HR
No swimming, hot tubs, baths, or soaking for 48 hours. Shower is fine — keep dressing dry. No soaking the incision site.
1WK
No strenuous lower body exercise involving glute/hip for 5–7 days. Walking is fine. No squats, lunges, cycling classes, running — increases extrusion risk.
DRESSING
Keep pressure dressing in place 24–48 hours. Remove Steri-strips after 5–7 days or allow to fall off naturally. Do not pull.
EXPECT
Mild bruising and tenderness normal for 5–7 days. Small firm lump at insertion site normal — this is the pellet. Resolves as pellet absorbs (3–6 months).
ONSET
Women feel benefit: 2–4 weeks. Men: 4–6 weeks for full effect. Sleep often improves first (week 1–2), libido and energy follow. Full effect at 6–8 weeks.
CALL IF
Increasing pain after day 3. Redness spreading beyond insertion site. Fever. Yellow/green discharge. Pellet visibly protruding through skin (extrusion).
⚠️ Complications & Management
EXTRUSION
Pellet extrusion (2–5% incidence): Pellet works its way to skin surface. Risk factors: too superficial placement, early strenuous exercise, infection. Management: wound care, allow to extrude naturally or minor procedure to remove. Document and adjust technique next insertion.
INFXN
Infection (<1%): Increasing pain, warmth, erythema, purulent discharge, fever. Treatment: remove pellet(s) if accessible, oral antibiotics (keflex or augmentin), wound care. Cultures if systemic signs.
HEMATOMA
Hematoma/significant bruising: More common in patients on anticoagulants. Pressure dressing immediately. Hold blood thinners 5–7 days pre-insertion when possible. Ice PRN. Usually self-resolving.
FIBROSIS
Scar tissue / fibrosis: Builds with repeated insertions at same site. Rotate sides every insertion. Document site used. Fibrosis makes insertion harder — use alternate location if significant resistance felt.
POLY
Polycythemia (men): Hematocrit >54%. Hold next pellet until Hct normalizes. Therapeutic phlebotomy if symptomatic (>58%). Reduce testosterone dose at next insertion. Monitor q3 months.
AROM
Excess aromatization (men): High E2, gynecomastia, water retention, mood changes. Add anastrozole 0.5–1mg 2x/week. Recheck E2 in 6 weeks. Target E2 <40 pg/mL in men.
VIRILIZE
Virilization (women): Acne, hirsutism, voice changes, clitoromegaly, hair thinning. Reduce testosterone dose at next insertion. Consider spironolactone for androgenic side effects. Cannot remove pellet — manage symptomatically.

🧠 High-Yield Clinical Pearls
★
Cannot remove a pellet once inserted. If patient has adverse reaction — manage symptomatically. This is the most important consent point.
★
Extrusion risk = superficial placement + early exercise. Place deep enough in subQ. Rest site for 7 days. Enforce exercise restriction.
★
Rotate insertion sides. Document which side. Fibrosis from repeated same-site insertions makes subsequent procedures harder.
★
Trough labs guide dosing — not peak. Draw 4 weeks BEFORE next scheduled insertion, not right after.
★
Men: watch Hct every cycle. Polycythemia is the most common serious side effect of testosterone pellets. Hold if Hct >54%.
★
Women feeling "wore off early" = underdosed, not interval too long. Increase dose — don't shorten interval below 3 months.
★
Mammogram current before every estrogen cycle in women. Document in chart. Non-negotiable for liability protection.
★
Consent must include: Cannot remove pellet, virilization risk (women), polycythemia risk (men), extrusion risk, infection risk, no guarantee of results.
✅ Contraindications
ABS-W
Women absolute: Breast cancer (current or history). Estrogen-sensitive cancers. Unexplained vaginal bleeding. Active VTE/PE. Pregnancy.
ABS-M
Men absolute: Prostate cancer (active). Breast cancer. Hematocrit >54% (defer until corrected). Severe BPH with urinary obstruction.
REL
Relative: Active skin infection at proposed site. Severe coagulopathy. Immunosuppression (infection risk). BRCA carriers — shared decision-making with OB/GYN.
CONSENT
Document all contraindications screened and cleared. Signed informed consent must specifically address irreversibility before every insertion.
