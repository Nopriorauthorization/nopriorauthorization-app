Peptide Therapy — Overview & Scope of Practice

What peptides are, who can prescribe them, and why this is the fastest-growing category in wellness

Critical — the regulatory landscape is actively shifting

Peptide therapy exists in a complex and rapidly evolving regulatory space. Several peptides previously available through compounding pharmacies have been reclassified or restricted by the FDA in recent years — including BPC-157, TB-500, and others. Before building a peptide program, verify the current FDA and DEA status of every peptide you intend to prescribe. The status of compounded peptides changes frequently. This playbook reflects the general landscape as of 2025 — always verify current status with your compounding pharmacy and legal counsel before prescribing.

What peptides are — and why patients want them

Peptides — the basics

Peptides are short chains of amino acids — the building blocks of proteins — that act as signaling molecules in the body. Unlike hormones that replace a deficient hormone directly, peptides work by signaling the body to increase its own production of hormones, growth factors, or repair proteins. They are used therapeutically to support: growth hormone optimization, fat loss, muscle preservation, injury recovery, immune modulation, cognitive function, sleep quality, skin regeneration, and sexual function. The specificity of peptide signaling — acting on targeted receptors — is what makes them clinically interesting and commercially appealing.

Scope of practice — who can prescribe

Prescribing authority for peptides

  • MDs, DOs — full prescribing authority in all states
  • NPs — independent prescribing authority in full-practice states; collaborative agreement required in others
  • PAs — prescribing authority varies by state; collaborative agreement typically required
  • Estheticians, RNs without prescriptive authority, medical assistants — cannot prescribe peptides
  • Peptides that are compounded require a valid patient-provider relationship and a legitimate clinical indication — not cosmetic use alone

Patient Screening & Candidate Selection

Who is right for peptide therapy — and how to open the conversation

Opening consultation script — peptide inquiry
"Tell me what's driving your interest in peptides — what are you hoping to feel or achieve that you're not experiencing right now?"
Peptide patients often arrive with significant self-research. Meet them where they are. Understand their goals before recommending anything. This question surfaces the right peptide category — body composition, recovery, skin, sleep, or cognitive function — without leading them.
Ideal peptide therapy candidates — by goal

Body composition and anti-aging

  • Adults 35+ experiencing gradual muscle loss, increasing body fat despite stable lifestyle
  • Athletes and active patients seeking improved recovery and lean mass
  • Patients with documented low IGF-1 levels — growth hormone secretagogues most indicated here
  • GLP-1 patients looking to preserve muscle mass during weight loss — peptides complement well

Recovery and healing

  • Patients with chronic musculoskeletal injuries not responding to standard treatment
  • Post-surgical patients (with surgical team awareness and approval)
  • Athletes with overuse injuries or slow recovery
  • Patients with gut dysfunction or inflammatory conditions (BPC-157 historically used — check current FDA status)

Skin, hair, and aesthetic enhancement

  • Patients seeking collagen support beyond topical skincare
  • Post-procedure patients wanting enhanced healing and skin quality
  • Hair loss patients — GHK-Cu has shown promise in hair follicle stimulation
  • Patients wanting systemic skin benefits alongside their injectable aesthetic treatments

Sleep, cognition, and stress

  • Patients with sleep disruption not fully addressed by hormone optimization
  • Patients with stress-related fatigue, brain fog, and burnout
  • Patients seeking cognitive enhancement and neuroprotection
  • Patients interested in mood support alongside conventional care
Baseline evaluation — what to assess before prescribing

Recommended baseline labs for GH secretagogue programs

  • IGF-1 — the primary marker of growth hormone status; guides indication and monitors response
  • Fasting glucose and HbA1c — GH secretagogues can affect insulin sensitivity; baseline essential
  • Fasting insulin — metabolic baseline
  • CBC and CMP — general health baseline
  • TSH — thyroid function; overlapping symptoms and GH affects thyroid axis
  • Lipid panel — GH optimization can affect lipids; baseline and monitoring
Contraindications to consider

Use with caution or avoid

  • Active malignancy — growth-promoting peptides are generally contraindicated; consult oncology
  • Proliferative diabetic retinopathy — GH secretagogues may worsen
  • Uncontrolled diabetes — GH affects glucose; optimize diabetes first
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding — no safety data; avoid
  • Known hypersensitivity to peptide components
  • Active carpal tunnel syndrome — GH-related fluid retention can worsen
  • History of hormone-sensitive cancers — individualized risk discussion required

Key Peptides — Clinical Overview & Protocols

The most clinically relevant peptides for aesthetic and wellness practices

Always verify current FDA status before prescribing any compounded peptide

The FDA has reclassified several peptides as bulk drug substances that cannot be compounded. Status changes with little warning. Verify with your compounding pharmacy before building a protocol around any specific peptide.

Growth Hormone Secretagogues — body composition and anti-aging

Sermorelin

GHRH analog — stimulates pituitary GH release

PrescriptionFDA History

Clinical use and dosing

  • Stimulates pituitary to release natural GH — pulsatile, physiologic
  • Typical dose: 200–500mcg subcutaneously at bedtime
  • Cycle: 3–6 months minimum; often continued long-term
  • Results: improved sleep quality (often first), then body composition, energy
  • Well-tolerated — side effects generally mild (flushing, tingling)

Clinical pearls

  • Works best when combined with adequate sleep — GH releases during deep sleep
  • Inject at bedtime on empty stomach for best response
  • Monitor IGF-1 at baseline and 3 months
  • Do not inject with meals — insulin blunts GH release
  • Historically FDA-approved for pediatric GH deficiency — compounded for adults

Ipamorelin + CJC-1295

GHRP + GHRH combination — the most commonly prescribed GH secretagogue stack

PrescriptionCompounded

Clinical use and dosing

  • Ipamorelin: selective GHRP — stimulates GH release without significant cortisol or prolactin spike
  • CJC-1295 (without DAC): short-acting GHRH analog — synergistic with Ipamorelin
  • Combined dose: Ipamorelin 200–300mcg + CJC-1295 100–200mcg subcutaneous
  • Timing: at bedtime, 3–5x per week or daily
  • Cycle: 3–6 months; reassess IGF-1 before continuing

Expected outcomes timeline

  • Week 1–2: improved sleep depth and quality — most common first result
  • Week 3–4: increased energy, mild fat loss beginning
  • Month 2–3: body composition changes visible — lean mass, reduced visceral fat
  • Month 3–6: skin quality improvement, hair quality, recovery enhancement
  • Monitor: IGF-1 at 3 months. Fasting glucose quarterly.

Tesamorelin

FDA-approved GHRH analog — strongest evidence base

FDA ApprovedPrescription

Clinical use

  • Only FDA-approved GHRH analog currently available
  • FDA-approved for HIV-related lipodystrophy; prescribed off-label for body composition
  • Dose: 1–2mg subcutaneous daily
  • Strongest clinical evidence for visceral fat reduction of all GH secretagogues
  • Available commercially (Egrifta) — expensive without insurance

Why it matters

  • Most robust evidence base — ideal for evidence-conscious patients
  • Documented visceral fat reduction — appeals to metabolic health focus
  • Monitor glucose — can reduce insulin sensitivity
  • Good alternative when compounded CJC/Ipamorelin availability is limited
  • Pairs well with GLP-1 programs for enhanced body composition
Healing and repair peptides

BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound)

Systemic healing peptide — gut, tendon, ligament, nerve repair

Verify FDA StatusCompounded

Documented benefits

  • Accelerated tendon and ligament healing — strong animal data
  • Gut mucosal healing — inflammatory bowel, leaky gut support
  • Nerve regeneration support
  • Anti-inflammatory — systemic and localized
  • Routes: oral capsule (gut-focused), sublingual, or subcutaneous injection

  • Human clinical trial data is limited — most evidence from animal models
  • Do not make efficacy claims beyond what evidence supports

Sexual health peptides

PT-141 (Bremelanotide)

FDA-approved melanocortin receptor agonist for female sexual dysfunction

FDA Approved — VyleesiPrescription

Clinical use

  • FDA-approved (Vyleesi) for hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in premenopausal women
  • Dose: 1.75mg subcutaneous injection 45 minutes before anticipated sexual activity
  • Mechanism: acts centrally on melanocortin receptors — not vascular like PDE5 inhibitors
  • Also prescribed off-label for men with erectile dysfunction and low desire
  • Available commercially and through compounding pharmacies

Patient selection and counseling

  • Ideal for patients where low libido persists despite hormone optimization
  • Common side effects: nausea (most common), flushing, headache — transient
  • Avoid in patients with cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled hypertension
  • Monitor blood pressure — can transiently decrease BP then increase
  • Pairs naturally with testosterone optimization for women with HSDD
  • High satisfaction rates when patient is properly selected and counseled

Vendors, Sourcing & Quality Control

Where to source peptides, how to vet pharmacies, and what to avoid

Never source peptides from research chemical suppliers

A significant portion of peptides sold online are marketed as "research chemicals" and are not intended for human use. These products have no quality oversight, no purity testing, no sterility assurance, and no regulatory accountability. Prescribing or recommending these products to patients is both dangerous and potentially illegal. Only source from licensed compounding pharmacies with verifiable accreditation.

Vetting your compounding pharmacy — the checklist

Before you prescribe a single peptide — verify all of the following

  • PCAB accreditation or 503B FDA registration — non-negotiable
  • Certificate of analysis (COA) available for each batch — purity and potency verified by independent third-party testing
  • Sterility testing documentation for all injectable preparations
  • Endotoxin (pyrogen) testing for injectables
  • Current regulatory compliance — confirm each peptide you're prescribing is on their approved formulary and legally compoundable
  • Cold chain shipping capability — peptides are temperature-sensitive
  • Prescriber support line — you need to be able to reach a pharmacist with clinical questions
Patient sourcing — what to tell them
What to say when a patient asks about buying peptides online
"I understand the appeal — the internet is full of peptide sources that look legitimate. But the reality is that without a licensed compounding pharmacy, there's no way to verify what's actually in the vial. Purity, potency, sterility — none of that is guaranteed. The risk is real and it's not worth it. When we prescribe through an accredited pharmacy, you know exactly what you're getting and you have a provider supervising your care. That's not just a legal formality — it's the difference between a program that works safely and one that may not work at all."

Do's & Don'ts — Peptide Therapy in Practice

The clinical, operational, and legal rules that protect your patients and your practice

Do — always

  • Verify the current FDA compounding status of every peptide before prescribing
  • Use only PCAB-accredited or 503B FDA-registered compounding pharmacies
  • Request and review COA (certificate of analysis) for each batch
  • Obtain written informed consent before initiating any peptide program
  • Document the clinical indication for every prescription clearly
  • Obtain baseline labs appropriate for the peptide category being prescribed
  • Schedule 4-week follow-up and monitor for side effects and response
  • Train patients on proper injection technique if self-administering
  • Teach patients proper sharps disposal — OSHA compliance
  • Be honest with patients about evidence levels — emerging vs. established
  • Stay current on regulatory updates — FDA reclassifications happen without warning
  • Have consent forms reviewed by a healthcare attorney in your state

Don't — never

  • Source peptides from research chemical suppliers or unverified online sources
  • Prescribe or recommend peptides from sources without verified pharmacy accreditation
  • Make efficacy claims beyond what the evidence supports
  • Prescribe GH secretagogues to patients with active malignancy
  • Skip baseline labs to "save the patient money" — this is clinical and legal risk
  • Prescribe without a follow-up plan — abandonment liability
  • Continue prescribing a peptide whose compounding status has changed without verifying legality
  • Allow patients to self-source peptides and then "supervise" the program
  • Market peptide therapy as anti-aging cures or guaranteed fat loss
  • Prescribe outside your scope of practice or without required prescribing authority
  • Ignore patient-reported side effects — document every concern and your response
  • Stack multiple peptides without understanding interaction profiles
Building the peptide program into your practice — step by step
1

Start with one peptide category — not the entire formulary

Choose one area: body composition (GH secretagogues) OR healing (BPC-157 if available) OR skin (GHK-Cu). Master one category first. Build your protocols, consent forms, and patient education. Expand from a position of confidence, not overwhelm.

2

Establish your pharmacy relationship first

Before you see your first peptide patient, have your compounding pharmacy relationship established, your prescriber account set up, and your formulary confirmed. Nothing is more professionally embarrassing than promising a patient a protocol you can't yet deliver.

3

Add peptide screening to your existing intake forms

"Are you interested in peptide therapy for body composition, recovery, or anti-aging?" — one question surfaces interested patients from your existing base. You likely have patients right now who would sign up immediately.

4

Price it as a program, not a product

Monthly program fee ($200–$400) covers provider management. Peptide cost billed separately through pharmacy. Labs billed separately. This creates three revenue streams from one patient relationship and makes the ongoing monitoring feel included rather than additional.

5

Cross-sell deliberately — not aggressively

"Your GH secretagogue program is going to improve your body composition and skin quality over the next 3–6 months. A lot of patients doing this program love combining it with microneedling or filler — the skin responds better when the foundation is optimized." That's clinical, not sales.

Patient FAQs — Peptide Therapy

The questions every peptide patient asks — answered clearly and compliantly

What exactly are peptides and how are they different from hormones?▾
Peptides are short chains of amino acids — the building blocks proteins are made from. Your body naturally produces thousands of peptides that act as signaling molecules, telling cells and organs what to do. Unlike hormones, which replace a deficient hormone directly, therapeutic peptides work by signaling your body to increase its own production of hormones, growth factors, or repair proteins. Think of hormones as a replacement part and peptides as instructions that help your body make more of what it needs on its own. This signaling approach is one of the reasons peptides are so interesting therapeutically — they work with your body's existing mechanisms rather than replacing them.
Are peptides safe?▾
When prescribed by a licensed provider, sourced from an accredited compounding pharmacy, and used at appropriate doses with proper monitoring — peptides have a generally favorable safety profile. The risk increases significantly when peptides are sourced from unverified online suppliers with no quality controls. This is why we only prescribe through accredited pharmacies where every batch has a certificate of analysis confirming purity and potency. Like any medical treatment, peptides carry risks that we discuss fully before starting, and we monitor you throughout the program to catch any concerns early.
Do I have to inject peptides? Is there another option?▾
It depends on the peptide. Some peptides — like GH secretagogues — are most effective when injected subcutaneously because they're broken down in the digestive system before reaching the bloodstream. Others, like BPC-157 and GHK-Cu in some formulations, can be taken orally or applied topically with reasonable efficacy for certain indications. We'll discuss delivery options for your specific protocol at your consultation. If you're uncomfortable with injections, we'll find the most effective option that works for you — and if injection is the best route, we'll make sure you feel completely confident with the technique before you leave our office.
How long until I see results?▾
This varies significantly by peptide and by goal. GH secretagogues typically produce improved sleep quality within the first 1–2 weeks — often the first thing patients notice. Energy improvements typically follow in weeks 2–4. Body composition changes — actual changes in lean mass and fat — require 2–3 months minimum of consistent use. Skin and hair quality improvements from GH secretagogues or GHK-Cu tend to appear at 3–6 months. Healing peptides for injury often show results faster — 4–8 weeks in many cases. The honest answer is that peptides reward patience and consistency. We'll set realistic expectations based on your specific protocol at your consultation.
Can I take peptides if I'm already on hormone therapy?▾
In most cases, yes — and for many patients, combining hormone optimization with peptide therapy produces better results than either alone. Growth hormone secretagogues, testosterone, and estrogen each work through different mechanisms and often complement each other. For example, optimized testosterone levels improve the body composition response to GH secretagogues, and optimal estrogen levels support the skin benefits of GHK-Cu. We review your complete medication and supplement list before prescribing any peptide and flag any interactions or timing considerations. No combination is started without that thorough review.
Why can't I just buy peptides online? They're cheaper.▾
I understand the appeal — the price difference can be significant. But here's the reality: peptides sold as "research chemicals" online have no quality oversight. No purity testing. No sterility testing. No verification that what's in the vial is what the label says. Studies of unregulated peptide sources have found products that are mislabeled, underdosed, overdosed, or contaminated. You're injecting this into your body. The risk of infection, allergic reaction, or simply a product that doesn't work because it's not what it claims to be — that's not a risk worth taking to save money. When we prescribe through an accredited pharmacy, you have documentation of exactly what's in your vial. That's worth the difference in cost.
Will peptides help me lose weight?▾
Some peptides support body composition improvement — meaning they can help reduce body fat and preserve or build lean muscle — but this is different from the significant weight loss produced by GLP-1 medications. GH secretagogues like Ipamorelin/CJC-1295 improve the ratio of lean mass to fat, increase metabolic rate, and can reduce visceral fat over 3–6 months. This is most noticeable in patients who are already active and eating well. Peptides are not a replacement for GLP-1 therapy if significant weight loss is the goal — but they're an excellent complement, particularly for preserving muscle during GLP-1-induced weight loss. We'll discuss what's right for your specific goals at your consultation.
What does a peptide program cost?▾
Our peptide programs are structured as monthly management fees covering your provider visits, protocol adjustments, and ongoing support. The peptide itself is billed separately through our pharmacy partner. Labs are billed separately at initiation and monitoring visits. Total monthly investment typically ranges from $200–$600 depending on the peptide(s) prescribed and your monitoring needs. We'll give you a complete cost breakdown at your consultation before you commit to anything. Our goal is a program that delivers real results at a cost that makes sense for the long term — we want you to be able to sustain this.
