NormalBalanced oil-water ratio. Small pores. Minimal sensitivity."My skin is pretty low-maintenance. No major issues."Even tone, smooth texture, minimal lines, no visible poresMaintenance, prevention, SPF, antioxidants

Fitzpatrick Type — Ask This Question
Type IAlways burns, never tans. Very pale/ivory skin. Usually red or blonde hair.
Type IIUsually burns, rarely tans minimally. Fair/light beige skin.
Type IIISometimes burns mildly, tans gradually to light brown.
Type IVRarely burns, tans easily to moderate brown. Olive skin.
Type VVery rarely burns, tans easily to dark brown. Brown skin.
Type VINever burns, tans very deeply. Dark brown to black skin.
★ Ask: "What happens when you go in the sun without protection for 30 minutes?" — this is the most accurate classifier.
Glogau Photoaging Classification
Type I — Mild20s–30s. No keratoses. Minimal wrinkles. Little/no makeup needed.
Type II — Moderate30s–40s. Early keratoses. Smile lines. Foundation worn routinely.
Type III — Advanced50s+. Visible keratoses. Persistent wrinkles. Heavy foundation always worn.
Type IV — Severe60s–70s. Extensive actinic keratoses. Wrinkling throughout. Cannot wear makeup.
The 5 Analysis Zones — What to Look for in Each

Zone 1 — Forehead

Oiliness: T-zone begins here
Lines: Horizontal (frontalis), vertical (glabella)
Pigment: Melasma common here
Texture: Congestion, milia common
Note: Most sun-exposed zone

Zone 2 — Nose & Cheeks

Pores: Largest pores on nose
Oiliness: Nose = peak T-zone
Redness: Cheeks = rosacea zone
Capillaries: Visible on cheeks in sensitive types
Pigment: Sun damage on cheeks

Zone 3 — Periorbital

Skin thickness: Thinnest skin on face (~0.5mm)
Lines: Crow's feet (dynamic), under-eye lines
Dark circles: Vascular vs pigmented vs structural
Bags: Fat pad herniation or edema
Note: Most sensitive zone — gentle products only

Zone 4 — Perioral & Chin

Lines: Vertical lip lines, marionette lines
Acne: Hormonal acne concentrated here
Volume loss: Chin and perioral deflation with age
Texture: Peau d'orange on chin (mentalis)
Note: Hormonal changes show here first

Zone 5 — Neck & Décolletage

Laxity: Platysmal bands, skin laxity
Pigment: Sun damage, dyschromia common
Texture: Often neglected — shows age quickly
Lines: Horizontal neck lines, décolletage wrinkling
Note: Thinner, more reactive than facial skin

T-Zone vs U-Zone

T-Zone: Forehead + nose + chin — typically oilier
U-Zone: Cheeks + jaw border — typically drier
Combination skin = oily T, normal/dry U
Treat zones separately with combination skin
Note: Document zone differences in every chart
Common Skin Concerns — Visual ID & Treatment Direction

Hyperpigmentation

Melasma: Bilateral cheeks/forehead. Hormonal trigger. UV-worsened.
PIH: Follows acne or trauma. Darker in higher Fitzpatrick.
Solar lentigines: Sun spots. Discrete, flat, brown.
Treatment: SPF first always. Hydroquinone, kojic, azelaic, mandelic, IPL (I–III only).

Acne & Congestion

Comedones (open/closed): Blackheads/whiteheads. BHA + retinoids.
Inflammatory acne: Papules/pustules. Salicylic, BP, retinoids.
Cystic/nodular: Deep, painful. Refer for Rx (tretinoin, spiro, Accutane).
Fungal acne: Uniform tiny pustules. Responds to antifungals not standard acne tx.

Redness & Rosacea

ETR (Erythematotelangiectatic): Flushing, diffuse redness, capillaries.
PPR (Papulopustular): Looks like acne. Do NOT treat as acne.
Triggers: Heat, spicy food, alcohol, UV, stress, harsh products.
Treatment: Gentle barrier support, azelaic acid, centella, IPL for vessels (I–III only).

Aging & Volume Loss

Dynamic lines: With expression only. Botox.
Static lines: Present at rest. Filler + resurfacing + retinoids.
Volume loss: Hollowing cheeks, NLF, tear trough. Filler.
Laxity: Jowling, neck, perioral descent. RF, Morpheus8, threads, surgery.

Texture Issues

Enlarged pores: Cannot shrink pores — can reduce appearance. Retinoids, niacinamide.
Rough texture: Keratin buildup. AHAs, gentle exfoliation, retinoids.
Milia: Keratin-filled cysts. Extraction or retinoids to prevent.
Scarring: Atrophic (ice pick, boxcar, rolling) — microneedling, fractional laser.

Dehydration vs Dry

Dehydrated skin: Lacks WATER — any skin type can be dehydrated.
Dry skin: Lacks OIL — a true skin type condition.
Dehydration signs: Fine surface lines that disappear when skin is plumped. "Crepiness."
Treatment: Dehydration → hyaluronic acid. Dry → barrier repair + occlusives.
The 8 Questions — Every New Client Consultation
1. Main concern"What brings you in today? What bothers you most about your skin?"
2. Current routine"Walk me through what you're using morning and night."
3. SPF habits"Are you using sunscreen daily? Which one?"
4. Medications"Any prescription skincare? Oral medications? Accutane history?"
5. Reactions history"Have you ever reacted to a skincare product or treatment?"
6. Prior treatments"Any facials, peels, laser, injectables in the past 4 weeks?"
7. Goals / timeline"Is there a specific event or timeline you're working toward?"
8. Sun exposure"How much time do you spend in the sun? Any recent tan or self-tanner?"
Stop Before You Start — Universal Contraindications
❌ Accutane (isotretinoin): No waxing, peels, dermaplaning, or microneedling during use or 6–12 months after
❌ Active infection: No facial treatment over active acne cysts, cold sores, open wounds, or skin infections
❌ Recent injectables: No facial massage, cupping, or microcurrent over Botox/filler sites within 2 weeks
❌ Pregnancy: Avoid chemical peels, essential oils, retinoids, electrical modalities, and high-frequency
❌ Active rosacea flare: No steam, extractions, heat, or strong actives — barrier support only
❌ Photosensitizing medications: No laser, IPL, or UV exposure (doxycycline, tetracycline, certain antifungals)
