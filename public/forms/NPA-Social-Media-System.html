The Med Spa Social Media Strategy

Why consistency beats virality — and the exact framework that converts followers to patients

The brutal truth about med spa social media

Most providers post when they feel like it, stop when they get busy, and wonder why Instagram isn't bringing them patients. The algorithm does not reward talent. It rewards consistency. A provider who posts mediocre content three times a week beats a provider who posts perfect content once a month — every single time.

The one metric that actually matters

Stop chasing followers. Start tracking this.

The only number that matters on your med spa Instagram is profile visits to booking page clicks. Not follower count. Not likes. Not reach. If 1,000 people see your post and zero click to book — that post failed regardless of how many hearts it got. Every caption, every graphic, every story should have one job: move someone one step closer to booking.

The 4 content types — your entire strategy in one framework
1

Educational — 40% of your content

Teach something. Answer a question patients Google. Explain how a treatment works. Bust a myth. This builds trust and Google findability simultaneously. Educational content is shared more than any other type — which grows your audience for free.

2

Social proof — 30% of your content

Before/afters with consent. Patient testimonials. Review highlights. Results. This is what converts a follower who's been watching you for 3 weeks into someone who finally books. Never skip this category — it's your sales team working 24/7.

3

Behind the scenes — 20% of your content

Your face. Your team. Your space. Your day. People book providers they feel like they know. BTS content builds the relationship that converts. This is also the hardest content for competitors to copy because it requires you to actually show up as a human being.

4

Promotional — 10% of your content

Direct offers. Specials. New services. Open appointment slots. This should be the smallest percentage — but providers who never promote leave money on the table. One direct CTA post per week is appropriate. More than that and followers tune you out.

Compliance Rules for Med Spa Social Media

The FTC and FDA rules ChatGPT doesn't know — that protect your license and your business

This is your legal protection — read before you post anything

Medical aesthetic providers are held to stricter advertising standards than general businesses. Violating FTC or FDA advertising guidelines can result in warning letters, fines, and in serious cases, license implications. Every caption template in this system was written with these rules in mind. Here's what you need to know.

The non-negotiable rules
Rule❌ Never say this✓ Say this instead
No guaranteed results "Botox will eliminate your wrinkles" "Botox softens the appearance of wrinkles for most patients"
No cure or permanent claims "Permanently remove fat with cryolipolysis" "Reduce targeted fat cells — results are long-lasting with a healthy lifestyle"
Before/after photo rules Unequal lighting, makeup, or angle between photos Same lighting, angle, no makeup — add: "Individual results may vary"
Testimonial rules "My patients always see amazing results" Real patient quote + "Results vary. This patient's experience is her own."
Off-label claims "Botox cures migraines" (unless FDA-approved for that) Only claim FDA-approved indications in marketing materials
Pricing transparency Advertising "starting at $X" if nobody actually qualifies for that price "Starting at $X for [specific service or unit count]" — be specific
Before/after photo consent — required every time

Your photo release must cover

  • Explicit permission to use the photo on social media — not just "I consent to treatment"
  • Which platforms: Instagram, Facebook, website, Google
  • Whether you can tag the patient or keep them anonymous
  • Duration of consent and right to revoke in writing
  • Keep a signed copy in the patient chart — not just a digital checkbox

The disclaimer that protects you — add to every before/after post

"Individual results may vary. [Treatment] results depend on individual factors including age, skin type, lifestyle, and treatment plan. This patient's results are her own and are not guaranteed for all patients. Complimentary consultations available to discuss what's right for you."

The Batching System — One Day a Month, Done

How to create an entire month of content in 3 hours so you never post under pressure again

Why you're inconsistent — and it's not your fault

Providers don't fail at social media because they're lazy. They fail because they try to create content the same day they post it — when they're tired, between patients, with no ideas and no time. The batching system removes all of that. You create everything once a month in a focused session. Then you execute daily in under 5 minutes.

The monthly batching session — 3 hours, once a month
1

Hour 1 — Content planning (30 min) + Photo collection (30 min)

Open the 4-week calendar template. Fill in your specific topics for this month. Collect all photos you've taken this month — before/afters (consented), BTS shots, team photos, product photos. Drop them into a Canva folder.

2

Hour 2 — Caption writing (60 min)

Open this playbook. Pick the caption templates that match your content plan. Customize each one — add your city, your service name, your specific offer. 12 captions × 5 minutes each = 60 minutes. You now have two weeks of content written.

3

Hour 3 — Graphic creation (60 min)

Open your Canva template pack. Drop your photos into the pre-designed templates. Update the text. Export. 12 graphics × 5 minutes each = 60 minutes. Done.

4

Schedule everything — 20 minutes

Load all content into Later, Buffer, or Meta Business Suite (free). Schedule to post at 7–9pm on posting days. Turn on auto-publish. Walk away. Your content posts itself for the next 28 days.

Daily execution — 5 minutes per day

What you do each day after batching

  • Morning (2 min): Check comments from yesterday — respond to every one. Comments boost algorithm visibility significantly.
  • Midday (1 min): Post one story — a quick poll, a question box, or a photo from the day. Stories don't need to be perfect. They need to be real.
  • Evening (2 min): Engage with 5 other accounts in your niche — comment something real on their posts. This signals to Instagram that you're an active community member and boosts your own visibility.

The tool stack — all free or nearly free

Canva (free): design all graphics. Later (free up to 30 posts/mo): schedule posts. Meta Business Suite (free): schedule to Instagram and Facebook simultaneously. Google Sheets (free): your content calendar lives here. Total cost: $0. The only investment is 3 hours once a month.
