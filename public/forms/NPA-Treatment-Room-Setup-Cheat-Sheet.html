Injectable Treatment Room — Standard Setup
Patient chair/tableAdjustable treatment chair or table. Patient positioned appropriately for zone being treated (reclined for forehead, seated for lower face).
LightingBright, even, overhead lighting + magnification lamp or ring light for assessment. Natural-looking lighting for post-treatment review.
Sterile fieldSterile drape on instrument tray. All supplies within reach before patient enters room. Never reach over patient mid-treatment.
Injectable suppliesProduct (stored appropriately — Botox refrigerated), syringes (27–32g for toxin, 25–30g for filler), alcohol wipes, gauze, topical numbing, cannulas if using.
Emergency suppliesHyaluronidase IN THE ROOM (not a cabinet, not down the hall). Epinephrine auto-injector if allergic reaction possible. Emergency protocol posted visibly.
CameraClean iPhone or camera. Ring light. Photo backdrop or designated photo spot. Photo consent confirmed before any photos taken.
DocumentationTablet or laptop for charting. Pre-printed consent forms if not digital. Patient chart pulled up before patient enters.
Esthetic Treatment Room — Standard Setup
Table setupFresh linens for every patient. Head rest with clean cover. Blanket available. Patient comfort is a reflection of your practice.
Steam/warmthFacial steamer (if using) turned on and warmed 10 minutes before patient arrives. Warm towels if no steamer.
Products stagedAll products for the specific treatment laid out in order of use before patient arrives. Never "hunting" for products mid-treatment.
Tools sanitizedAll reusable tools (extractors, brushes, spoons) sanitized and in covered clean container. Disposable items in sealed packaging.
Microneedling specificMicroneedling device charged and set to correct depth for today's protocol. Topical anesthetic out. Serums for post-needling ready.
Post-care suppliesCool compress ready for post-treatment. Patient-specific post-care printed and ready at end of treatment.
IV Therapy Room — Standard Setup
IV standIV pole with bag hooks. Drip chamber and IV line primed with saline before patient enters. No air in the line.
IV supplies18–22g angiocatheter (based on patient and vein), tourniquet, alcohol wipes, tegaderm, gauze, IV tape, flush syringe (10mL normal saline).
IV bag / medicationsConfirm product, concentration, and patient against order before preparation. Label bag with patient name, date, contents, time prepared.
Monitoring suppliesBlood pressure cuff. Pulse oximeter. These should be checked pre-infusion and post-infusion for all patients.
Emergency suppliesEpinephrine auto-injector in the room. Benadryl IM available. Emergency protocol posted. Staff trained in anaphylaxis response.
Patient comfortComfortable reclining chair. Warm blanket. Entertainment (TV, tablet). IV therapy takes 1–4 hours — comfort is non-negotiable.
Laser / IPL Room — Standard Setup
Protective eyewearBOTH patient and provider eyewear BEFORE powering on the device. Wavelength-specific eyewear — confirm it matches the device being used.
Device calibrationCalibrate device per manufacturer protocol before first patient of the day. Check energy output, contact cooling, and spot size.
Skin prepClean skin — no lotions, sunscreen, or makeup in treatment area. Shave hair removal areas day before (not day of). Confirm Fitzpatrick type.
Test patchFor new patients or sensitive areas — test patch 24 hours before or same day (per protocol). Document settings and response.
CoolingContact cooling (built into device), cold gel, or chilled air cooling. Cooling reduces pain and protects epidermis — especially in darker skin.
Post-laser suppliesCool gel or cold compress ready immediately post-treatment. Aloe vera or calming serum. SPF 50+ for patient to apply before leaving.
Universal Room Standards — Every Room Every Day
✓ Surfaces wiped with hospital-grade disinfectant between every patient — treatment table, chair, equipment touchpoints
✓ Fresh linens / covers for every patient — never reuse. Cross-contamination risk.
✓ Sharps container within arm's reach during any procedure involving needles. Never recap needles.
✓ No food or drink in treatment rooms — infection control + professionalism
✓ Check expiration dates on all products and medications at the start of each week. Expired products are a liability.
✓ Document any adverse reaction or incident in the patient chart the same day. Never wait.
